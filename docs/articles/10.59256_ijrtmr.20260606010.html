# Summary: Sensory, Communicative, and Environmental Drivers of Disrobing in Autistic Adolescents The primary objective of this systematic review and meta-analysis was to identify sensory, communicative, and environmental factors associated with disrobing behavior in autistic adolescents and to evaluate evidence-based interventions for its prevention and management. The researchers conducted a systematic search across five databases (PubMed, PsycINFO, ERIC, Embase, and Scopus) using terms related to autism, adolescence, disrobing, sensory processing, and behavioral interventions, then assessed studies for methodological quality, behavioral function, and intervention outcomes through thematic synthesis and quantitative analysis where studies were sufficiently comparable. For BCBAs and autism practitioners, these findings underscore the importance of conducting thorough functional behavior assessments and implementing function-matched, individualized, and dignity-preserving interventions rather than applying uniform responses to disrobing behavior across all clients.

Abstract

Disrobing or clothing-removal behavior among autistic adolescents may be associated with sensory discomfort, communication needs, behavioral functions, and environmental conditions, yet direct evidence concerning this behavior remains limited. This systematic review and meta-analysis synthesized empirical literature examining sensory, communicative, and environmental factors associated with disrobing and evaluated evidence-based supports relevant to its prevention and management. A systematic search of PubMed, PsycINFO, ERIC, Embase, and Scopus was conducted using terms related to autism, adolescence, disrobing, clothing removal, sensory processing, functional behavior, communication, and intervention. Eligible evidence was assessed according to study characteristics, methodological quality, behavioral function, intervention type, and reported outcomes. Findings were synthesized thematically, with quantitative analysis considered only for sufficiently comparable studies. The evidence identified three interconnected domains: sensory, communicative/behavioral, and environmental factors. Sensory findings indicated that tactile over-responsivity, clothing materials, garment features, and broader sensory load may contribute to clothing-related discomfort and avoidance. Behavioral evidence demonstrated that clothing removal may also serve social or communicative functions, including attention and escape from demands, emphasizing the importance of functional assessment. Environmental factors, including task demands, transitions, predictability, and social consequences, were also relevant. Evidence-based supports included functional communication training, alternative communication, antecedent modification, visual supports, reinforcement, and individualized sensory or clothing accommodations. Overall, the findings support individualized, function-matched, dignity-preserving interventions rather than a uniform explanation or response to disrobing
