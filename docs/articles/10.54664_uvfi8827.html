Summary

# Summary: AAC Outcomes in Bulgarian Children with ASD This study examined whether a six-month individualized, multimodal AAC intervention would improve communicative competence in children with autism spectrum disorder. The researchers used a quasi-experimental single-group pre-test/post-test design with 30 Bulgarian children aged 5–15 years, administering the Communication Matrix, Communication Complexity Scale, Goal Attainment Scaling, a PECS phase checklist, and the Social Communication Questionnaire to measure outcomes. Paired-sample analyses revealed statistically significant gains across all three primary measures, with the largest effects observed in intentional-symbolic communicative behavior and frequency of spontaneous requests; age, non-verbal cognitive level, and intensity of parental involvement were identified as the strongest moderators of progress. These findings support the international evidence base that AAC intervention does not inhibit speech development and actively supports expressive, social-pragmatic, and behavioral functioning in children with ASD. BCBAs implementing AAC interventions should consider that parental involvement intensity, child age, and cognitive level are key factors influencing treatment outcomes and should be monitored and adjusted accordingly.

Abstract

Augmentative and Alternative Communication (AAC) has become one of the most evidence-supported intervention approaches for minimally verbal and non-verbal children with Au tism Spectrum Disorder (ASD). The present study examined the effects of a six-month individualised, multimodal AAC intervention on the communicative competence of 30 children with ASD aged 5–15 years. A quasi-experimental single-group pre-test/post-test design was used. Three freely available, internationally validated instruments were applied — the Communication Matrix [1], the Commu nication Complexity Scale (CCS) [21] and Goal Attainment Scaling (GAS) [2]— supplemented by a PECS phase checklist and the Social Communication Questionnaire (SCQ) for baseline charac terisation. Paired-sample analyses revealed statistically significant gains across all three primary measures, with the largest effects observed in intentional-symbolic communicative behaviour and in frequency of spontaneous requests. Age, non-verbal cognitive level and the intensity of parental involvement emerged as the strongest moderators of progress. The findings are consistent with in ternational evidence showing that AAC does not inhibit speech development and actively supports expressive, social-pragmatic and behavioural functioning, and they add to the still limited Bulgarian empirical literature on AAC.
