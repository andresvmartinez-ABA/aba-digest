# Summary: Adaptive Cyber-Secure Small Doll for Autism Support The study aimed to develop a cyber-secure robotic system embedded within a plush doll to support therapeutic interventions for children with autism spectrum disorder, particularly addressing challenges in social interaction and emotional expression. The researchers designed and implemented a prototype integrating facial recognition technology, proximity sensors, servo motors for animated responses, Python-based emotional recognition using colored LED feedback, and a secure caregiver notification system via email, all controlled through an Arduino micro-controller. The abstract does not report specific quantitative results, effect sizes, or outcome data from participant testing; rather, it describes the technical specifications and features of the prototype system as developed. For BCBA practitioners, this tool may offer potential as an adjunctive aid for delivering remote supervision during therapy sessions and providing real-time visual feedback on emotional recognition, though empirical evidence regarding its efficacy in improving social skills or emotional expression in children with ASD would be needed to inform clinical decision-making.

Abstract

Children with autism spectrum disorder (ASD) often face significant challenges in social interaction and emotional expression, frequently favouring engagement with objects over direct human communication. This project presents an adaptive, cyber-secure robotic system embedded within a plush doll, designed to support therapeutic interventions for children with ASD. The prototype integrates a facial camera for real-time face and eye tracking, proximity sensors for presence detection, and servo motors to simulate lifelike responses to the child's gaze. Emotional recognition is performed using Python-based image processing, with coloured LEDs indicating detected affective states, thereby offering visual feedback. A caregiver notification mechanism was developed using secure email communication to improve remote supervision during therapy. Hardware integration includes a display panel, a built-in camera, sensors, and actuators, all of which are controlled via an Arduino micro-controller. The implementation of the system lies in combining emotional interactivity, physical embodiment, and data privacy protections in a child-friendly form, developing a valuable tool for personalised autism support therapy.
