Summary

# Summary: AI-Driven Game Interventions for Children with ASD This systematic review and meta-analysis examined the effectiveness of artificial intelligence-driven game interventions for children with autism spectrum disorder. The researchers searched eight databases through November 2025 following PRISMA 2020 guidelines and conducted a three-level meta-analysis to evaluate the intervention outcomes. Results demonstrated that AI-based games produced a significant positive overall effect on children with ASD (Hedges' g = 0.528, p < .001), with the strongest effects observed for interventions targeting combined attention and social skills. The study identified four core gamification elements—feedback, rewards, real-life scenarios, and levels—that appear to drive intervention effectiveness and provide guidance for developing future AI-driven game interventions. These findings suggest that AI-driven games warrant consideration by BCBAs and autism practitioners as a potential complementary tool for addressing attention and social skills deficits, though practitioners should evaluate individual applicability and fidelity when implementing such interventions.

Abstract

To date, gamified interventions for children with autism spectrum disorder (ASD) have diversified. With advances in artificial intelligence (AI) technology, an increasing number of intervention games incorporate AI to enhance therapeutic outcomes. However, existing systematic reviews and meta-analyses primarily focus on evaluating the effectiveness of interventions across various treatment goals. While some studies assess application outcomes based on different game categories, few systematically examine AI-driven games from this perspective. This study followed the PRISMA 2020 guidelines to retrieve literature on AI-driven game interventions for children with ASD from eight databases up to November 2025, subsequently employing a three-level meta-analysis approach for evaluation. Results indicate that AI-based games produce a significant overall positive effect on children with ASD (Hedges'g $=0.528, p<.001)$, with the most pronounced effects observed for combined attention and social skills intervention goals. Furthermore, the study dissected the sources of heterogeneity and identified four core gamification elements (feedback, rewards, real-life scenarios, and levels), providing systematic guidance for future research.
