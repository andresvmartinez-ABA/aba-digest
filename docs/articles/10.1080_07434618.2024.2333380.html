A demonstration of incorporating discriminative stimuli into an AAC device during functional communication training.

Angley, Shannon M, Mitteer, Daniel R, Greer, Brian D et al.  ·  Augmentative and alternative communication (Baltimore, Md. : 1985)  ·  2024
COMPARING DDTTs (Delay to Reinforcement Tolerance Treatments)
85/100This article directly aligns with multiple core interests including AAC devices, functional communication training, discriminative stimuli, and autism interventions, though it involves an adult participant which slightly reduces relevance.
Summary

This study examined the effectiveness of incorporating discriminative stimuli into an augmentative and alternative communication (AAC) device during functional communication training (FCT) to teach appropriate timing of communication requests. The researchers used single-case experimental designs with a non-vocal autistic adult to teach them to make requests only when reinforcement was available, as signaled by the color of AAC icons. The intervention successfully taught the participant to discriminate when to communicate based on icon color cues for both tangible and edible items, with stimulus control rapidly transferring to new icons and both stimulus classes being integrated into a single AAC grid. This study represents the first empirical demonstration of embedding discriminative stimuli into an AAC device, showing promise for individuals who do not use speech and may not respond well to traditional delay or denial cues.
