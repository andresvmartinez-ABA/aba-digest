Assessing preference for types of social interaction.

Samuel L. Morris, Timothy R. Vollmer  ·  Journal of applied behavior analysis  ·  2019
95/100 Manually curated paper from Research Rabbit library.
Summary

This study aimed to develop and evaluate a new method for assessing preference for social interactions in individuals with autism spectrum disorder, addressing limitations of existing stimulus preference assessment methods when applied to social reinforcers. The researchers conducted a social interaction preference assessment (SIPA) and a concurrent operant reinforcer assessment with five participants diagnosed with autism spectrum disorder. The study successfully identified differentially preferred and reinforcing social interactions for all five participants, demonstrating the effectiveness of the SIPA methodology. The findings suggest that the SIPA provides a viable approach for systematically assessing social interaction preferences in this population.

Abstract

To date, few researchers have evaluated methods for assessing preference for social interactions. Due to concerns that commonly used stimulus preference assessment methods may be inappropriate, or at least cumbersome, for the assessment of social reinforcers, we developed and evaluated a new method of assessing preference for social interactions. A social interaction preference assessment (SIPA) and a concurrent operant reinforcer assessment were conducted with five participants diagnosed with autism spectrum disorder. A differentially preferred and reinforcing social interaction was identified for all five participants. The SIPA procedures, results, and the implications of these results are discussed.
