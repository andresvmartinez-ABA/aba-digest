Experience and Development Needs for Tablet PC-Based Applications to Improve Communication Skills of Children with Autism Spectrum Disorder

Min Ji Kim, Seok Jeong Yeon, Ki-Hyung Hong  ·  Korean Society of Augumentative and Alternnative Communication  ·  2025

Summary

# Summary: Tablet PC-Based Applications for Communication in Children with ASD This study explored caregivers' experiences and identified developmental needs for tablet-based applications designed to improve communication skills in children with autism spectrum disorder. The methodology involved qualitative interviews with six mothers of nonverbal children with ASD (ages 3–5 years) who had used the AVATA-AAC application for at least six weeks; interview transcripts were analyzed thematically. Three primary themes emerged from the data: caregiver experiences with the application, the need for age- and ability-appropriate application features, and requirements for improved usability, which were further subdivided into twelve subthemes. BCBAs and autism practitioners should consider these findings when recommending or implementing tablet-based AAC interventions, ensuring that applications address both parental expectations and practical usability constraints in real-world settings. This study underscores the importance of involving caregivers in the development and refinement of digital communication tools to maximize effectiveness and acceptability for young children with ASD.

Abstract

Background and Objectives: The purpose of this study was to explore experiences and developmental needs of using a tablet PC-based application to improve communication skills in children with autism spectrum disorder(ASD). Methods: Participants were six caregivers of children with ASD aged 3 to 5 years who were nonverbal or had an expressive vocabulary of 50 words or less with experience of using AVATA-AAC application, a tablet PC-based AAC intervention program, for at least six weeks. One-on-one in-depth interviews were conducted with these children's mothers. Transcripts were then analyzed. Results: Interviews were categorized into three main themes: experience with the application, need for age- and ability-appropriate application development, and need for application usability. These themes were further subdivided into twelve subthemes. Conclusion: This study highlights high expectations and needs of parents for digital therapies and limitations of developing applications for children with ASD.
