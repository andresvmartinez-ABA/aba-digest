# Summary: Teaching Turn Taking to Autistic Children Using an Individualized Virtual-Reality Game This study investigated the effectiveness of a personalized virtual-reality game in teaching turn-taking skills to children with autism. The intervention used an iterative design process grounded in applied behavior analysis, with game features adjusted to match each participant's individualized reinforcers and sensory needs. Four of five participants with Level 2 autism demonstrated improvements in turn-taking skills following gameplay, though one participant withdrew due to sensory overload. The findings suggest that individualized VR-based interventions may be effective for children who have not responded adequately to traditional teaching approaches. The authors emphasize that close collaboration between software developers and therapists during intervention design is critical for achieving successful outcomes in VR-based behavioral interventions.

Abstract

This paper presents a personalized virtual-reality (VR) game designed to teach turn taking to children with moderate-to-severe autism. Integrating applied behavior analysis, the game design was iteratively adjusted to accommodate individualized reinforcers and sensory needs. In an experimental evaluation, four of five participants with Level 2 autism demonstrated breakthroughs in turn-taking skills after engaging in gameplay, with one participant withdrawing due to sensory overload. The results provide promising evidence for the effectiveness of personalized VR-based interventions, especially on children for whom traditional approaches fail. Our experience indicates that the individualized approach in the iterative design flow is the key to our success. This calls for closer collaborations between software developers and therapists in the design of such VR-based interventions.
