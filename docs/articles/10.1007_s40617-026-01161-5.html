Access to Arbitrary Stimuli During Fixed-Lean Schedule Thinning.

Melanson, Isaac J, Fahmie, Tara A, Livingston, Cynthia P  ·  Behavior analysis in practice  ·  2026
FTF Authors
100/100[FTF author +25 → 100] Directly relevant as it addresses behavioral assessment and treatment, reinforcement schedule manipulation, delay to reinforcement tolerance, and challenging behavior maintained by tangible/escape reinforcement, with strong clinical application focus.
Summary

# Summary: Access to Arbitrary Stimuli During Fixed-Lean Schedule Thinning This study investigated whether continuous access to attention (an arbitrary stimulus unrelated to the function of challenging behavior) during fixed-lean schedule thinning would affect treatment outcomes for individuals whose challenging behavior was maintained by tangible reinforcement or tangible plus escape reinforcement. The researchers compared two conditions during reinforcement schedule thinning: sessions with continuous access to attention and sessions without attention, using a within-subject design with multiple participants. Results showed that challenging behavior reduced similarly across both conditions, with no significant differences in treatment efficacy; however, two of three participants and one caregiver expressed preference for the condition that included continuous access to attention. For BCBAs, this research suggests that incorporating arbitrary stimuli such as attention during fixed-lean schedule thinning may enhance treatment acceptability and social validity without compromising the effectiveness of behavior reduction, potentially making intervention implementation more feasible in naturalistic settings.

Abstract

Fixed-lean schedule thinning involves programming the terminal reinforcement schedule from the onset of schedule thinning rather than gradually reducing the delay to reinforcement over time in a dense-to-lean approach. This study extended Sumter et al. (2020) and investigated the effect of continuous access to attention (an arbitrary stimulus; not functionally related to challenging behavior) during fixed-lean schedule thinning for individuals who engaged in challenging behavior maintained by tangible reinforcement or a combination of tangible reinforcement and escape. In some sessions, participants received continuous access to attention during reinforcement schedule thinning, and in others they waited without attention. Results demonstrated a similar reduction in challenging behavior across both conditions. However, two participants, along with the third participant’s caregiver, preferred the condition that included attention. This study expands upon existing research by examining the application of arbitrary stimuli within fixed-lean schedule thinning, suggesting that continuous access to attention may enhance treatment acceptability without disruptions to treatment efficacy. • Fixed-lean schedule thinning programs the terminal reinforcement schedule from the start and may be more efficient than other schedule thinning approaches. • Researchers have explored alternatives to traditional extinction during reinforcement schedule thinning, such as the addition of an arbitrary stimulus. • Access to an arbitrary stimulus can help sustain low levels of challenging behavior during reinforcement schedule thinning. • Access to an arbitrary stimulus may be preferred over no access.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1007/s40617-026-01161-5.
