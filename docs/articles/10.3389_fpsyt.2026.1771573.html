Immersive wearable virtual reality for autism: a systematic review of current evidence.

Failla, Chiara, Minutoli, Roberta, Chilà, Paola et al.  ·  Frontiers in psychiatry  ·  2026
TECHNOLOGY IN ABA
78/100Highly relevant systematic review on immersive VR technology for autism assessment and intervention in children and adults, directly matching core interests in technology-assisted interventions and communication outcomes, though somewhat limited by inclusion of adult populations and lack of focus on specific behavioral treatment approaches.
Summary

# Summary: Immersive Wearable Virtual Reality for Autism This systematic review examined the current evidence for using immersive and wearable virtual reality (VR) technology to support assessment and intervention for autistic individuals, addressing the need to synthesize findings across heterogeneous studies in this emerging field. The authors conducted a PRISMA-compliant systematic review with narrative synthesis, searching five databases for studies published between 2015 and August 2025, ultimately including 22 studies that investigated wearable and immersive VR interventions with children and adults with ASD. Across the included studies, wearable VR interventions demonstrated potential to improve social communication, joint attention, emotional regulation, daily living skills, executive functioning, and user engagement; additionally, technologies incorporating eye-tracking and artificial intelligence enabled objective measurement of gaze behavior, social interaction, and physiological responses. However, the evidence base is significantly limited by considerable methodological heterogeneity, predominantly small sample sizes, minimal use of randomized controlled designs, and insufficient long-term follow-up data, which substantially reduces generalizability of findings. For BCBA practitioners, the authors recommend implementing structured pre-intervention assessments that integrate sensory, cognitive, emotional, and VR tolerance profiles to guide individualized intervention planning, while emphasizing that future research must establish standardized outcome measures, employ rigorous designs, and conduct longitudinal studies to support clinical translation of VR-based interventions in autism.

Introduction

Immersive and wearable virtual reality (VR) is an emerging technology with growing potential to support assessment and intervention ifor autistic people. The methodological heterogeneity of existing studies limits the interpretation and generalization of current evidence.

Methods

A systematic review with a narrative synthesis was conducted in accordance with the PRISMA guidelines. Electronic searches were performed in PubMed, Scopus, IEEE Xplore, Web of Science, and Google Scholar, identifying studies published between 2015 and August 2025. Twenty-two studies investigating wearable and immersive VR interventions in children and adults with ASD met the eligibility criteria.

Results

The included studies demonstrated that wearable VR interventions may improve social communication, joint attention, emotional regulation, daily living skills, executive functioning, and user engagement. Innovative technologies, including eye-tracking and artificial intelligence-based systems, also enabled objective assessment of gaze behaviour, social interaction, and physiological responses. Nevertheless, the evidence was characterized by considerable methodological heterogeneity, predominantly small sample sizes, limited use of randomized controlled designs, and scarce long-term follow-up, reducing the generalizability of the findings.

Discussion

Wearable VR represents a promising tool for personalized assessment and intervention in ASD. Based on the current evidence, we propose a structured pre-intervention assessment integrating sensory, cognitive, emotional, and VR tolerance profiles to support individualized intervention planning. Future research should prioritize standardized outcome measures, rigorous study designs, and longitudinal investigations to strengthen the clinical translation of VR-based interventions in autism.
