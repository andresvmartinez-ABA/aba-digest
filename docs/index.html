This Week

Hey there — this week's digest leans heavy into technology in ABA, with 14 articles exploring how our field is adapting tools and tech to improve practice, so there's plenty to dig into if that's on your radar. We've also got a couple of pieces on automatic negative reinforcement that are worth a slow read, plus a nice head-to-head comparison of DDTT variations and a study looking at stereotypy as reinforcement alongside functional living environment considerations. Field Pulse

AI & Technology in ABA — Recent Advances

AI-Assisted Speech & Language Therapy

Recent work in AI-assisted speech and language therapy continues to extend into augmentative and alternative communication (AAC) supports for autistic learners, as seen in Pope, Holyfield, DeLuca, Schweizer, Jakobs, and colleagues' study on integrating an AAC literacy feature into shared storybook reading, which examined how an AAC topic display influences the accuracy with which children with autism or autism-like characteristics who need or rely on AAC identify letter–sound correspondences, including measuring participants' ability to select the target picture on the AAC system during shared reading sessions.

Social Robots

Recent work continues to focus on robot-assisted interventions for children with autism spectrum disorder, approaching the topic from engineering, ethical, and clinical-validation angles. Qiu et al. describe a multi-source perception-fused intelligent intervention robot system that pairs the Picture Exchange Communication System with a humanoid robot for social skills training, using cognitive load as the core metric for evaluating a child's teachability. In contrast, Elmadagli raises ethical questions about robot-assisted therapies and socially assistive robots that use AI to target socio-emotional and communicative skills, probing whether such systems can genuinely be considered ethical given trends toward semi-autonomous operation. AAC & Communication Devices

Recent work continues to expand the evidence base and practical scope of AAC interventions across different populations and settings. Mushambi's study examines how AAC systems can be leveraged as strategies for identifying and advancing career pathways for learners with high-functioning autism, while also highlighting persistent barriers such as limited AAC resources, insufficient training among educators, and gaps in understanding autism-specific needs, as voiced by parent participants seeking further guidance. In a related but distinct focus on younger children, Ousley and colleagues investigate the combined effects of naturalistic developmental behavioral intervention strategies and visual scene displays on joint engagement in autistic children, emphasizing the importance of introducing AAC even when a child's need for it is not immediately apparent, and of remaining attentive to trauma and processing considerations during intervention.

Virtual & Augmented Reality Interventions

Two newly identified studies extend the evidence base on immersive technology for autism support. Feasibility and acceptability of therapist-guided immersive virtual reality for autism rehabilitation in Nepal presents a rapid qualitative study drawing on interviews with caregivers of autistic children and an autistic child's own participation in a therapist-guided VR session, examining how such technology might fit within multidisciplinary autism services that already include communication and behavioral interventions in a low-resource setting.
