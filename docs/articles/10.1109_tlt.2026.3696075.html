Summary

# Summary: Robots in Inclusive Education of Students With Autism Spectrum Disorder This study examined how humanoid and social robots (NAO, Pepper, and KASPAR) affect the development of social and communication skills in inclusive educational settings for children with autism spectrum disorder. The researchers used a mixed-methods design with 30 children with ASD who engaged with robots over a 12-week period, measuring social sensitivity and communication skills through pre- and post-intervention assessments. Children demonstrated significant improvements in non-verbal communication and social interaction following robot-mediated intervention, with stronger outcomes correlating with better social adaptation during robot interactions. For BCBA practitioners and educators, these findings suggest that humanoid robots may serve as supplementary tools to support social and communication skill development in inclusive classroom environments. The study provided practical recommendations for teachers implementing robotics in instruction, policymakers addressing equitable technology access, and robotic system developers designing interventions tailored to the needs of students with ASD.

Abstract

The study examined how humanoid and social robots affect social and communication skills in inclusive education for children with autism spectrum disorder (ASD). A mixed-methods approach collected and analyzed data using quantitative and qualitative methods. The study included 30 children with ASD who used NAO, Pepper, and KASPAR robots for 12 weeks. The main task was to diagnose children's social sensitivity and communication skills before and after using robots in education to evaluate this approach. Children's social adaptation and robot-mediated interaction were associated with significant improvements, particularly in non-verbal communication and social interaction. Robots can make learning more inclusive and supportive, according to the study. The study examined robotic therapy development theory, including pros and cons. The analysis focused on successful ASD teaching methods in Norway, Japan, Bulgaria, and the US, where inclusive education and the latest technologies are used to support students with special needs. Based on the data, practical recommendations have been developed for teachers on how to integrate robots into teaching, policymakers on equal access to technology, and developers of robotic systems that focus on the needs of children with ASD.
