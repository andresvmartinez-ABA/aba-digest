Exploration of the Application of AI Virtual Training in Special Education: A Case Study of Social Skills Development for School aged Autistic Children Aged six-twelve

He Wang, Chen Zhang, R. Zeng  ·  Proceedings of the 2026 2nd International Conference on Artificial Intelligence and Educational Systems  ·  2026

Summary

# Summary: AI Virtual Training for Social Skills in Autistic Children The study aimed to explore whether AI-based virtual training can address limitations of traditional special education in developing social skills for school-aged autistic children (ages 6–12), particularly regarding social communication deficiencies and perceptual difficulties. The researchers conducted a logistic regression analysis on 518 samples to examine which features of AI virtual training systems influenced user adoption, using computer graphics, adaptive reinforcement learning, and computer vision perception as core technical components. Key findings indicated that three functions significantly improved users' willingness to engage with the training: stimulating participation interest (strongest effect), providing a safe and controllable environment, and enabling repeatable scenario training, though specific effect sizes or outcome metrics were not detailed in the abstract. For BCBAs and autism practitioners, AI virtual training represents a supplementary tool for social skills instruction that can provide standardized, personalized, and low-pressure practice environments, though current technical limitations prevent it from replacing human-led intervention. The authors recommend an "AI-assisted plus human-led" integrated approach as special education evolves toward more precise and personalized interventions.

Abstract

The traditional special education model has shortcomings in intervening in children with autism aged six-twelve years old, such as outdated educational concepts, rigid teaching models, generalized curriculum systems, and one-sided evaluation systems. It is difficult to adapt to their core obstacle needs, such as social communication deficiencies and perceptual abnormalities. AI virtual training relies on computer graphics, multimodal perception hardware, deep learning frameworks, real-time rendering engines, and other computer technologies. Combined with core algorithms such as dynamic noise reduction, scene replication, adaptive reinforcement learning, and computer vision perception, it can construct low-pressure training scenarios, achieve standardized iterative training, carry out personalized interventions, and decompose social signals, effectively filling the limitations of traditional interventions. The logistic regression analysis based on 518 samples showed that functions such as stimulating participation interest, safe and controllable environment, and repeatable scenario training significantly improved users' willingness to choose, among which stimulating participation interest had the strongest impact. AI virtual training is a new auxiliary tool for the cultivation of social skills in children with autism. Although there are technical bottlenecks such as insufficient scene generalization and limited emotional interaction, it cannot replace human intervention. However, relying on the iteration of cutting-edge computer technologies such as multimodal large models, emotional computing, and real-time 3D simulation, combined with the “AI assisted+human led” fusion mode, it can promote the transformation of special education towards precision, personalization, and full humanization, and provide technical support for the improvement of social skills and social integration of children with autism.
