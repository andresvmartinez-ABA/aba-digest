Development of an Integrated Aided Language Stimulation (ALS) Intervention Model to Improve Expressive Communication in Autistic Children at UPTD Disability Services Sidoarjo

Summary

# Summary: Integrated Aided Language Stimulation Intervention Model for Autistic Children This study aimed to develop an integrated Aided Language Stimulation (ALS) intervention model combined with Augmentative and Alternative Communication (AAC) to improve expressive communication skills in autistic children at a disability services center in Indonesia. The researchers used a Research and Development approach with the Four-D model (Define, Design, Develop, Disseminate) and integrated multiple components including AAC media, language modeling, functional activities, communication opportunities, partner responsiveness, reinforcement, and generalization strategies. A limited trial with five autistic children showed pretest-posttest improvement in expressive communication scores from a mean of 4.2 to 7.6 (out of 100), with a mean N-Gain of 0.65 indicating moderate improvement; expert validation reached 90.5% (very valid), teacher/therapist practicality ratings were 90% (very practical), and implementation fidelity was 91% (very good). BCBAs and autism practitioners should note that while the model demonstrated feasibility and promising preliminary outcomes, the small sample size (n=5) and absence of a control group limit conclusions about intervention effectiveness, and larger-scale, rigorous comparative studies are needed before definitive clinical recommendations can be made. The integrated model's multi-component design and strong validation scores suggest potential value for practitioners seeking evidence-informed approaches to communication intervention, pending additional research.

Abstract

Children with autism spectrum disorder (ASD) often experience difficulties in expressive communication, including requesting, responding, expressing needs, initiating interaction, and maintaining communication. This study aimed to develop an Intervention Model Integrated Aided Language Stimulation (ALS) with Augmentative and Alternative Communication (AAC) to support expressive communication among autistic children at UPTD Services Disability Sidoarjo, Indonesia. The study employed a Research and Development approach using the Four-D model comprising Define, Design, Develop, and Disseminate stages. The developed model integrated AAC media, ALS language modeling, functional communication activities, communication opportunities, partner responsiveness, reinforcement, generalization, and evaluation. Expert validation involved four validators representing four areas of expertise: subject matter, special education/autism, communication/AAC, and media. The validation percentage was calculated by dividing the obtained score by the maximum possible score and multiplying by 100%, resulting in 90.5% (very valid). Practicality was assessed through responses from teachers, therapists, and/or companions using the same percentage formula, resulting in 90% (very practical), while implementation was assessed through structured observation and reached 91% (very good). A one-group pretest-posttest limited trial involving five autistic children showed an increase in expressive communication scores from 42/100 (mean = 4.2) to 76/100 (mean = 7.6). The mean N-Gain was 0.65, categorized as moderate improvement according to the study criteria. Because the limited trial involved only five children and did not include a control group, the observed improvement should not be interpreted as definitive evidence of intervention effectiveness. Overall, the findings support the model’s feasibility for further investigation with larger samples and more rigorous comparative designs.
