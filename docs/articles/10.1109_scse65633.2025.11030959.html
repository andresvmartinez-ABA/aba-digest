Summary

# Summary: Intelligent Dialog System for Sinhala-Speaking Children with ASD The study aimed to develop a conversational AI dialog system integrated with a NAO socially assistive robot to support speech therapy and social communication skills in Sinhala-speaking children with Autism Spectrum Disorder. The intervention utilized a pipeline-based architecture implemented through the RASA framework, incorporating Natural Language Understanding, Dialog Management, and Natural Language Generation components, with specialized tools for processing spoken Sinhala and dynamic conversation flow adaptation through rule-based and machine learning policies. The system engaged children through interactive dialogues on topics such as toy preferences, designed to enhance social interaction and communication capabilities in the target language population. The findings demonstrate the feasibility of integrating conversational AI and socially assistive robotics as a therapeutic approach for ASD in low-resource language contexts, though specific quantitative outcome measures (e.g., pre-post communication gains, effect sizes) are not detailed in the abstract. The practical implication for BCBAs is that technology-assisted interventions using culturally and linguistically tailored systems may expand access to evidence-based social-communication support for underserved populations, though rigorous controlled trials would be needed to establish efficacy and inform implementation decisions.

Abstract

This paper presents a dialog system integrated with a NAO socially assistive robot, designed to support Sinhala-speaking children with Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD). The system leverages a pipeline-based architecture implemented using the RASA framework, consisting of Natural Language Understanding (NLU), Dialog Management (DMU), and Natural Language Generation (NLG) units. The NLU unit processes user input by identifying intents, entities, and dialogue acts, incorporating custom tools like the SpokenSinhalaVerbTokenizer for handling spoken Sinhala. The DMU includes a Dialog State Tracker (DST) to maintain conversation context and a Dialog Policy Generator, which employs rule-based, TED, and UnexpecTED policies to adapt conversation flows dynamically. The NLG unit generates natural responses to foster interactive and goal-oriented conversations. Integrated with the NAO robot, the system engages children through meaningful dialogues, such as discussing toy preferences, aiming to enhance social interaction and communication skills. This work highlights the potential of conversational AI and robotics in therapeutic interventions for ASD in low-resource languages.
