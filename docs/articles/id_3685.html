Towards Automated Clinical Behavioral Coding with Large Language Models: A Case study Using BOSCC recordings of Children

J. Prescott, Aditya Kommineni, Tian-Tian Feng, Megan Micheletti, Alyssa Viggiano, Luis Angeles et al.  ·  2026
TECHNOLOGY IN ABA
72/100Highly relevant as it addresses technology-assisted assessment of social communication outcomes in children with autism using AI/LLMs, directly aligning with interests in technology and autism, social-communication measurement, and clinical application, though the focus is on automated coding rather than intervention delivery.
Summary

# Summary: Automated Clinical Behavioral Coding with Large Language Models The study aimed to evaluate whether large language models (LLMs) could automate the coding of speech-related behaviors from the Brief Observation of Social Communication Change (BOSCC), a validated measure of treatment response in autism intervention. The researchers compared LLM performance across three input conditions (transcripts, diarized transcripts, and targeted audio) using 163 recordings of children, analyzing the models' ability to predict BOSCC codes related to social communication. LLMs demonstrated strong performance in assessing verbal exchange but showed notably weaker performance in identifying atypical speech patterns, with considerable variability across different scoring decisions and no consistent performance differences across diagnosis groups. Model audits revealed that applying BOSCC coding criteria and interpreting ambiguous speech evidence remained significant challenges for the automated systems. These findings suggest that while LLMs show promise for automating certain aspects of BOSCC coding, current limitations in detecting atypical speech and applying nuanced clinical criteria indicate that full automation is not yet feasible for clinical BOSCC assessment by practitioners.

Abstract

Autism spectrum disorder (ASD) is a neurodevelopmental condition characterized by differences in social communication and by restricted interests and repetitive behaviors. Treatment interventions often target social-communication skills, creating a need for reliable measures of behavioral change. The Brief Observation of Social Communication Change (BOSCC) is a validated treatment-response measure based on brief play and social-communication interactions between a child and trained examiner. The BOSCC coding process is resource-intensive and requires trained experts, motivating the automation of coding in order to improve scalability and accessibility. In this work, we evaluate general-purpose large language models (LLMs) for predicting speech-related BOSCC codes from different input representations. We compare transcript, diarized-transcript, and targeted audio conditions across 163 in-house recordings. LLMs are able to perform well in assessing verbal exchange, but do not perform as well when identifying atypical speech patterns. Additionally, performance varies considerably across scoring decisions, with no consistent pattern across diagnosis groups. An audit of model predictions indicates that applying the BOSCC coding criteria and interpreting ambiguous speech evidence remain challenges.
