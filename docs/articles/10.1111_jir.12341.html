Problem behaviour in adolescent boys with fragile X syndrome: relative prevalence, frequency and severity.

Hall, S S, Barnett, R P, Hustyi, K M  ·  Journal of intellectual disability research : JIDR  ·  2016
SIB PREDICTORS
75/100This article directly addresses severe problem behaviors including self-injury, aggression, and stereotypy in a pediatric/adolescent population, which aligns well with the profile's clinical interests in behavioral assessment and problem behavior analysis, though it focuses more on prevalence data than treatment methodology.
Summary

This study investigated the relative prevalence, frequency, and severity of problem behaviors in adolescent boys with fragile X syndrome (FXS) compared to boys with mixed-etiology intellectual disability. The researchers collected survey data from caregivers of 85 boys with FXS and 155 age-matched boys with mixed-etiology intellectual disability, all of whom exhibited at least one form of problem behavior. The key findings revealed that boys with FXS showed significantly higher prevalence of stereotypy, more frequent self-injury, and less severe aggression compared to the mixed-etiology group. Additionally, the frequency of self-injury decreased with age in boys with FXS, and aggression and property destruction were highly correlated in both groups. These results suggest that the behavioral phenotype of FXS differs from general intellectual disability populations in specific patterns of problem behavior expression.
