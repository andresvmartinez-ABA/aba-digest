Motivating Individuals with Autism Spectrum Disorder to Talk with an Conversational Android Robot System Changing Context Awareness

Summary

# Summary: Motivating Individuals with ASD to Talk with a Conversational Android Robot System This study investigated whether an autonomous open-domain dialogue android robot system could increase motivation for conversation among individuals with autism spectrum disorder (ASD) by allowing flexible topic selection. The researchers developed an android robot capable of generating both context-aware and context-free utterances and conducted a conversational experiment in which ASD participants interacted with the system and rated their impressions and preferences for different utterance types. Results demonstrated heterogeneous preferences for context awareness across ASD participants, with each participant showing significantly higher motivation to engage in further conversations when using their individually preferred robot utterance type. These findings suggest that customizing robot dialogue features to match individual preferences may enhance engagement and motivation for social communication practice with robotic systems among individuals with ASD. BCBAs and autism practitioners considering robotic interventions for social communication might benefit from assessing individual learner preferences for interaction styles and personalizing robot communication features accordingly to optimize motivation and adherence.

Abstract

Android robot applications for individuals with autism spectrum disorder (ASD) to help learn social communication skills are widely investigated. To provide individuals with ASD with more opportunities and maintain motivation for regularly talking with android robots, these androids need to allow individuals to talk about anything at any time. This study introduces an autonomous open-domain dialogue android robot system to conversations with individuals with ASD, to investigate its potential to motivate them to talk. We employ the android robot system that is capable of generating two types of utterances, context-aware and context-free, and conduct a conversational experiment. Each participant talks with our robot system and assesses the impressions and preference for different types of robot utterances. The experimental results indicate that preferences for context awareness in robot utterances differ among ASD participants, and with their preferred system, each participant has a significantly higher motivation to have further conversations.
