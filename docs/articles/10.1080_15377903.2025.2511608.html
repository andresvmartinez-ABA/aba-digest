Comparative Effects of an Electronic Visual Schedule on Young Children with ASD

L. L. Tompkins, David M. Hulac, Amber E. Boetel  ·  Journal of Applied School Psychology  ·  2025
VISUAL SCHEDULES & TRANSITIONS
78/100Directly relevant study examining electronic (iPad-based) visual schedules for elementary-aged children with ASD, addressing technology-assisted communication tools, transition support, and behavioral outcomes in a school-age population using single-subject methodology.
Summary

# Summary: Comparative Effects of an Electronic Visual Schedule on Young Children with ASD The study examined whether an iPad-based visual schedule would be more effective than an analog visual schedule for improving independent task engagement and reducing transition latencies in young children with ASD. Using a single-subject ABCABC design, three elementary-aged participants were exposed to three conditions: no visual schedule, an analog visual schedule, and an electronic iPad-based visual schedule, with researchers measuring number of activities completed, prompts needed before task initiation, and latency between initial prompt and task onset. Two of the three students demonstrated increased independent activity-following behavior during the iPad condition, and all three students showed decreased latency to task initiation when using either type of visual schedule compared to verbal prompts alone. These findings suggest that both analog and electronic visual schedules can reduce transition delays in children with ASD, with electronic schedules potentially offering additional benefits for some learners' independent task engagement. BCBAs may consider electronic visual schedules as an evidence-based tool for improving independence and reducing prompt dependency during transitions, though individual student responsiveness should guide implementation decisions.

Abstract

For students with Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD), independent activity completion and transitions may prevent students from engaging in important activities. The current study compared an iPad-based visual schedule to an analog visual activity schedule. Across a single-subject ABCABC design, three elementary-aged children with ASD completed tasks with no activity schedule, an analog activity schedule, and an electronic activity schedule. The researchers counted the number of activities completed and prompts delivered before task initiation along with the amount of time between the first prompt and task initiation. Two students showed an increase in independent activity schedule following-behavior during the iPad phase. All students showed a decrease in latency when using either type of schedule as compared to hearing verbal prompts only.
