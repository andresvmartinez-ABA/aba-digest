Parenting in the early years and self-harm in adolescence: The role of control and reward systems in childhood.

Dawe-Lane, E, Flouri, E  ·  Journal of affective disorders  ·  2023
AUTOMATIC NEGATIVE REINFORCEMENT
75/100This article directly addresses self-injurious behavior with behavioral mechanisms (emotion regulation, delay aversion, reinforcement timing) that align well with the BCBA's interests in automatic reinforcement, delay tolerance treatments, and the underpinnings of self-injury, though it focuses on adolescents rather than the preferred preschool/school-age populations.
Summary

This study investigated whether early parenting practices contribute to adolescent self-harm through childhood emotion regulation and decision-making pathways using longitudinal data from the Millennium Cohort Study (n=11,145). Researchers assessed parenting measures (conflict, closeness, discipline) at age 3, tracked emotional regulation trajectories from ages 3-7 through latent growth curve analysis, evaluated decision-making using the Cambridge Gambling Task at age 11, and examined self-harm outcomes in adolescence through mediation models. The study found no direct association between early parenting and adolescent self-harm, but identified indirect effects where parenting conflict and closeness influenced self-harm through increasing emotional dysregulation trajectories over time. Additionally, delay aversion (preference for smaller immediate rewards over larger delayed rewards) at age 11 was positively associated with adolescent self-harm. The findings suggest that early identification and intervention for children with chronic emotional dysregulation and impulsive decision-making patterns may help prevent later self-harm behaviors.
