ERICA: An AI-Based Humanoid Robot for Early Intervention Therapy in Children with Autism Pervasive Development Disorder

P. Rusimamto, M. S. Zuhrie, Vivi Kurnia Herviani, Afrizal Luthfi Eka Arnatha, Dwi Aqidah  ·  2026 Ninth International Conference on Vocational Education and Electrical Engineering (ICVEE)  ·  2026

Summary

This study evaluated ERICA, an AI-powered humanoid robot designed to deliver early intervention therapy for children with autism spectrum disorder by providing adaptive social communication, cognitive, and motor skill support. The intervention used a Machine Learning-based system architecture incorporating facial recognition via convolutional neural networks (CNN) and multimodal inputs (push-button activity monitoring and camera-based facial analysis) to generate real-time adaptive robot responses including movements, sounds, and emotional visualizations. The system demonstrated overall accuracy of 91.3% across three interaction conditions (with individual accuracies ranging from 86.7% to 93.3%), and the machine learning module achieved an F1-score of 87.3% (precision: 88.2%; recall: 86.4%), though face detection performance was lower at 86.7% accuracy. For BCBAs and autism practitioners, ERICA represents a technology-based tool with proof-of-concept performance that could potentially supplement traditional therapy approaches and address resource limitations, though further evaluation of clinical efficacy and effectiveness with actual client populations would be necessary before implementation recommendations. The authors identified vision system improvements as a priority for future development to enhance the robot's reliability in real-world therapeutic applications.

Abstract

Autism Pervasive Development Disorder (APDD) poses significant challenges in early childhood development, particularly in social communication, cognitive growth, and motor skill acquisition. Conventional therapy approaches often face resource limitations and lack adaptability to individual child profiles. This paper presents ERICA (Useful Robot Therapy for Children with Autism), an AI-powered humanoid robot designed for early intervention therapy targeting children with APDD. ERICA integrates a Machine Learning-based perception system capable of processing multi-modal inputs including push-button activity monitoring and camera-based facial recognition. The system architecture employs a convolutional neural network (CNN) for facial expression analysis, enabling ERICA to generate adaptive movements, sounds, and emotional visualizations in real time. Experimental evaluation across three interaction conditions yielded an overall system accuracy of 91.3%, with individual condition accuracies of 93.3%, 86.7%, and 90.0%. The machine learning module achieved an F1-score of 87.3%, with precision of 88.2% and recall of 86.4%, demonstrating solid proof-of-concept performance. Although the face detection condition exhibited a slightly elevated failure rate of 13.3%—exceeding the 10% threshold—this finding identifies a clear direction for future improvement in the vision subsystem. ERICA represents a novel contribution toward scalable, technology-driven early intervention for children with APDD, bridging robotics, artificial intelligence, and pediatric therapy.
