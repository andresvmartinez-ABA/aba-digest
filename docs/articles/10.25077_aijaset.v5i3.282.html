Summary

# Summary: Informatics-Driven Therapeutic Innovations in Autism Spectrum Disorder This systematic review examined informatics-driven therapeutic approaches—including videogame-based interventions, extended reality (XR), and artificial intelligence systems—to address the sensory, cognitive, and behavioral challenges associated with Autism Spectrum Disorder. The review analyzed 34 peer-reviewed studies published between 2012 and 2025, evaluating the role of Health Information Technology (Health IT) infrastructures in the design, interoperability, and clinical validation of digital therapeutics for ASD. Results indicated that 76% of the analyzed studies reported statistically significant pre–post intervention improvements in social, motor, or cognitive performance, with technical systems commonly employing FHIR-based interoperability, SNOMED CT semantic encoding, and HIPAA/GDPR-compliant data pipelines. BCBAs and autism practitioners should consider that while digital therapeutics show promise, current limitations—including data scarcity, insufficient large-scale randomized trials, usability constraints, and incomplete electronic health record (EHR) integration—warrant cautious implementation and further validation before widespread clinical adoption. The authors recommend that effective ASD care delivery requires unified Health IT ecosystems that integrate AI, serious gaming, and extended reality within secure, interoperable, and ethically governed informatics frameworks to support scalable clinical practice.

Abstract

Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) presents multifactorial challenges in diagnosis, therapeutic delivery, and long-term care coordination. Informatics-driven therapeutic approaches including videogame-based interventions, extended reality (XR), and artificial intelligence (AI)-enhanced systems have emerged as promising modalities to address the sensory, cognitive, and behavioral complexity of ASD. This systematic review consolidates findings from 34 peer-reviewed studies (2012–2025), emphasizing the role of Health Information Technology (Health IT) infrastructures in the design, interoperability, and clinical validation of digital therapeutics.Results demonstrate that 76% of the analyzed studies report statistically significant pre–post intervention improvements in social, motor, or cognitive performance. Technical architectures commonly rely on FHIR-based interoperability, SNOMED CT semantic encoding, and HIPAA/GDPR-compliant data pipelines. Limitations include data scarcity, lack of large-scale randomized trials, usability constraints, and incomplete EHR integration. The review concludes that scalable ASD care requires unified Health IT ecosystems embedding AI, serious gaming, and extended reality through secure, interoperable, and ethically governed informatics frameworks.
