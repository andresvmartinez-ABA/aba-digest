Summary

# Summary: Tea-Trevés to Communicate in Primary School The study aimed to improve communication skills and promote classroom inclusion for first- and second-grade students with Autism Spectrum Disorder using a structured teaching approach. The researchers implemented the REAPSES method—which incorporates four learning areas (active, theoretical, reflective, and pragmatic)—across two primary schools in Zaragoza, utilizing Augmentative and Alternative Communication (AAC) systems, Information and Communication Technologies (ICT), and created instructional materials including visual supports and manipulative resources. The results indicated that students demonstrated improvements in attention and activity retention following implementation of the intervention activities. The authors concluded that the REAPSES method supports more individualized and motivating learning experiences that enhance communicative development in students with ASD. For BCBAs and autism practitioners, this research suggests that multimodal, individualized instructional approaches combining AAC, technology, and visual supports may facilitate communication skill development and engagement in primary-school-age children with ASD, though additional quantitative outcome data would strengthen clinical application recommendations.

Abstract

In primary education, we find children with Special Educational Needs (SEN) in our classrooms. Therefore, it is necessary to understand their developmental stages and the characteristics and levels of first-grade students with Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD). This study focuses on a teaching approach aimed at improving the communication skills of first- and second-grade students with ASD and promoting student inclusion through different activities developed using the REAPSES method, using four learning areas: active, theoretical, reflective, and pragmatic. These activities are implemented in two schools in Zaragoza. These activities involve oral and written learning using Augmentative and Alternative Communication systems (AAC) and Information and Communication Technologies (ICT). This has resulted in the creation of two books entitled "The world of letters"and "Discover and learn," as well as visual and manipulative materials. The results show that students can improve their attention and activity retention. The manuscript concluded that the REAPSES method promotes more individualized and motivating learning, which enhances effective communicative development.
