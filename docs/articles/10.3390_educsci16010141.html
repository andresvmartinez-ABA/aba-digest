School-Based Immersive Virtual Reality Learning to Enhance Pragmatic Language and Social Communication in Children with ASD and SCD

Julrode, Phichete, Puritat, Kitti, Ariya, Pakinee et al.  ·  Education Sciences  ·  2026
85/100This article directly aligns with the profile's interest in virtual reality interventions for children with autism, focusing on social communication outcomes in school-age populations with practical implementation in educational settings.
Summary

This study investigated whether a school-based immersive virtual reality (VR) program could improve pragmatic language and social communication skills in Thai children with Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) and Social Communication Disorder (SCD). Eleven participants aged 7-12 years completed a three-week, ten-session VR program that simulated culturally appropriate classroom, playground, and canteen interactions aligned with Thai sociocultural norms. The researchers measured outcomes using the Social Communication Questionnaire (SCQ) and the Pragmatic Behavior Observation Checklist (PBOC). Results showed no significant changes in overall social communication (SCQ), but significant improvements were observed in three specific pragmatic domains: Initiation and Responsiveness, Turn-Taking and Conversational Flow, and Politeness and Register. The findings suggest that culturally adapted immersive VR environments may support specific pragmatic language skills, though broader social communication improvements may require longer or more intensive interventions.

Abstract

Pragmatic language is a core component of school-based social participation, yet children with Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) and Social Communication Disorder (SCD) frequently experience persistent difficulties in using language appropriately across everyday learning contexts. This study investigated the effectiveness of a culturally adapted, school-based immersive Virtual Reality (VR) learning program designed to enhance pragmatic language and social communication skills among Thai primary school children. Eleven participants aged 7–12 years completed a three-week, ten-session VR program that simulated authentic classroom, playground, and canteen interactions aligned with Thai sociocultural norms. Outcomes were measured using the Social Communication Questionnaire (SCQ) and the Pragmatic Behavior Observation Checklist (PBOC). While SCQ scores showed a small, non-significant reduction (p = 0.092), PBOC results demonstrated significant improvements in three foundational pragmatic domains: Initiation and Responsiveness (p = 0.032), Turn-Taking and Conversational Flow (p = 0.037), and Politeness and Register (p = 0.010). Other domains showed no significant changes. These findings suggest that immersive, culturally relevant VR environments can support early gains in core pragmatic language behaviors within educational settings, although broader social communication outcomes may require longer or more intensive learning experiences.
