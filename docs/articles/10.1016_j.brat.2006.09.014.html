Thought suppression and self-injurious thoughts and behaviors.

Najmi, Sadia, Wegner, Daniel M, Nock, Matthew K  ·  Behaviour research and therapy  ·  2007
AUTOMATIC NEGATIVE REINFORCEMENT
75/100This article directly addresses self-injurious behavior with a focus on negative reinforcement mechanisms (escape from aversive emotions), which aligns well with the profile's interest in automatically reinforced self-injurious behavior and automatic negative reinforcement, though it focuses on adolescents rather than the preferred preschool/school-age populations.
Summary

This study examined whether the tendency to suppress unwanted thoughts is associated with increased self-injurious thoughts and behaviors (SITB) and serves as a cognitive mediator between emotional reactivity and SITB. The researchers used a cross-sectional design with 87 adolescents who completed self-report measures assessing thought suppression tendencies, emotional reactivity, and various forms of SITB including non-suicidal self-injury (NSSI), suicidal ideation, and suicide attempts. Results showed that higher propensity for thought suppression was significantly associated with increased presence and frequency of NSSI, suicidal ideation, and suicide attempts. Additionally, thought suppression partially mediated the relationship between emotional reactivity and frequency of NSSI and suicidal ideation, and adolescents with greater thought suppression tendencies were more likely to engage in NSSI for emotion regulation rather than social communication purposes.
