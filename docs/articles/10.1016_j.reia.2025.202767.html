A longitudinal investigation of aggression and social skills in autistic youth

Hannah M. Rea, Anika A. Lindley, Waylon Howard, E. Neuhaus, S. Faja, Raphael A. Bernier et al.  ·  Research in autism  ·  2025

Summary

# Summary: A Longitudinal Investigation of Aggression and Social Skills in Autistic Youth This study examined the longitudinal interrelations between aggression and social skills in autistic children, seeking to understand how these behaviors change and relate to each other over time rather than examining static correlations. The researchers used parallel process growth modeling and regression analysis on data from 280 autistic children (ages 6–11) assessed at three time points across six months, with aggression measured via the Aberrant Behavior Checklist (ABC) and social skills measured via the Vineland Adaptive Behavior Scales-Third Edition (VABS-3). Key findings indicated that aggression towards others decreased and social skills improved over the study period, self-injurious behaviors (SIB) remained unchanged, and while initial levels of social skills, aggression towards others, and SIB were intercorrelated, baseline scores did not predict subsequent changes in any domain. For BCBA practitioners, these results suggest that interventions targeting aggression in school-aged autistic children should address aggression towards others, SIB, and social skills as distinct behavioral targets, as improvements in one area may not automatically produce improvements in others.

Abstract

Objective:Autism is characterized by differences in social functioning and many autistic individuals exhibit co-occurring features, such as aggression. While previous research has examined correlations between aggression and social skills cross sectionally, research is needed to better understand the interrelations between these behaviors over time.Method:In a sample of 280 autistic children (ages 6-11 years), parallel process growth modeling and regression were used to examine changes in social skills and aggression across three time points, as well as the interrelations between social skills and aggression. A confirmatory factor analysis (CFA) was also conducted on the measure of aggression (the Aberrant Behavior Checklist), which yielded separate factors for aggression towards others and self-injurious behaviors (SIB).Results:Over time, aggression towards others decreased and social skills improved, but SIB did not change. While initial levels of social skills, aggression towards others, and SIB were inter-related, there were no significant relations between initial scores and subsequent changes in any domain.Conclusions:Interventions for aggression in school-aged autistic children should consider aggression towards others, SIB, and social skills as separate targets, as changes in one domain did not appear to be related to changes in other domains.

Methods

Data were collected as part of the Autism Biomarkers Consortium for Clinical Trials (ABC-CT). Data collection occurred at the University of Washington, Duke University, University of California, Los Angeles, Yale University, and Boston Children’s Hospital/Harvard University. The study was approved by the research ethics committee or institutional review board at Yale University. Parents of participants provided consent and children provided assent. Data collection occurred at three time points (T1, T2 six weeks later, T3 six months later). Further details about the larger protocol for ABC-CT and characteristics of the sample are available (Faja et al., 2023; McPartland et al., 2020). Data are available via NDA (study #2288) and the project is listed in ClinicalTrials.Gov (NCT02996669).

Participants between the ages of 6 through 11 years were enrolled in the ABC-CT study across five sites. Of the 399 youth who participated, 280 were autistic. Only autistic youth were included in the current analysis. For the autistic group, children had a research diagnosis of autism on the Autism Diagnostic Observation Schedule-2 (Lord et al., 2021), the Autism Diagnostic Interview-Revised (Rutter et al., 2003), and the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual, Fifth Edition (American Psychiatric Association, 2013; see Faja et al., 2023). Full-scale IQ was confirmed to be between 60 to 150 using the Differential Abilities Scale-II (Elliott et al., 2018) and those with an IQ below 60 were excluded. Participants and caregivers were English-speaking. Children were excluded from the larger ABC-CT study if they had a known genetic or neurological condition, history of epilepsy, clinically significant visual or auditory impairment, sensory-motor difficulties that would impact use of diagnostic tools, a history of significant prenatal, perinatal, or birth problems, neonatal brain damage, or a known severe environmental exposure. Children taking neurological or psychiatric medications were included if they were on a stable regimen for eight weeks prior to their first visit. At T1, 101 children had ABC-I raw scores ≥ 15, which is the commonly used cutoff for inclusion in clinical trials for interventions related to aggression. Demographics are presented in Table 1.

Social skills were measured using the socialization composite score of the Vineland Adaptive Behavior Scales-Third Edition (VABS-3) (Sparrow, 2011). The VABS-3 is a semi-structured parent interview about the child’s adaptive functioning. The Socialization composite includes Interpersonal Relationships, Play and Leisure, and Coping subscales. Raw scores are converted to standard scores based on chronological age with a mean of 100 and a standard deviation of 15. Administrators demonstrated at least 90% item-level reliability with the lead clinician prior to data collection (Faja et al., 2023).The VABS-3 had good test-retest reliability (ICC=0.94).

Aggression was measured using the Aberrant Behavior Checklist (ABC) (Aman et al., 1985). The ABC is a 58-item questionnaire with five subscales: Irritability (15 items), Social Withdrawal (16 items), Stereotypy (7 items), Hyperactivity (16 items), and Inappropriate Speech (4 items). Items are rated on a 4-point Likert scale (0=not at all a problem, 3=the problem is severe in degree). The ABC was created to measure problem behaviors for individuals with developmental disabilities The Irritability subscale (ABC-I) items about tantrums, aggression, and SIB were used in the CFA to determine the measure of aggression.

All analyses were conducted using R (Version 4.4.0; R Core Team, 2024) and Mplus (Version 8.10; Muthén & Muthén, 1998-2023). Preliminary analyses: A confirmatory factor analysis (CFA) was first used to evaluate the measurement properties of aggression items from the ABC-I across three time points. Measurement invariance was established across time, indicating that the measure was capturing the same construct the same way at each time point. This allowed for the computation of latent factor (i.e., variables) scores that could be used in subsequent analyses. Aim 1: Latent growth models (LGMs) were then used to examine trajectories of social skills, aggression, and SIB over time. This model estimates where individuals start in each domain and how those levels change over time. Aim 2: A parallel process LGM assessed interrelations among growth parameters, and covariates (age, IQ, sex assigned at birth) were added to examine their predictive effects. A parallel process model estimates all three LGMs simultaneously to identify how social skills, aggression, and SIB trajectories are related over time. Model fit was evaluated using standard SEM indices, and missing data were handled with full information maximum likelihood (FIML). See the Supplementary Materials for more detailed information on the data analytic plan (S1) and for information on missing data (S2). The Supplementary Materials (S3) also contain a power analysis that indicates that the current sample had sufficient power to detect effects in both a CFA and a parallel process model with or without covariates.

Results

Over time, aggression towards others decreased and social skills improved, but SIB did not change. While initial levels of social skills, aggression towards others, and SIB were inter-related, there were no significant relations between initial scores and subsequent changes in any domain.

Discussion

Interventions for aggression in school-aged autistic children should consider aggression towards others, SIB, and social skills as separate targets, as changes in one domain did not appear to be related to changes in other domains.
