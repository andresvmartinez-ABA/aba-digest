Punishment happens, but the state of behavior analysis is changing for the better

S. Ferrier, Mirela Cengher, A. Clearfield  ·  Behavioral interventions  ·  2025
SOCIAL VALIDITY
72/100Highly relevant as it directly addresses behavior analytic treatment procedures, functional assessment methodology, social validity evaluation, and the evolution of ABA practice—all core interests in the profile—though it focuses on punishment procedures rather than the specific reinforcement-based or problem behavior treatments most emphasized.
Summary

# Research Summary The primary research question was whether contemporary behavior analytic literature continues to rely heavily on restrictive punishment procedures, updating previous criticism of the field's historical practices. The researchers conducted a systematic review of 59 peer-reviewed studies published between 2014 and 2023, extracting data on participant characteristics, procedural characteristics, and treatment efficacy to document trends in punishment procedures used in behavior analytic research. Key findings indicated a decrease in physical forms of punishment over the decade reviewed, an increase in the use of response interruption and redirection procedures, and increased reporting of social validity, generalization, and maintenance outcomes; however, there was a concurrent decrease in functional assessment of target behaviors across studies. For BCBAs and autism practitioners, these findings suggest the field is shifting toward less restrictive procedures and more comprehensive evaluation of treatment outcomes, though the decreased reliance on functional assessment warrants attention to ensure interventions remain function-based and appropriately tailored to individual clients. The authors provide recommendations for future research, clinical practice, and advocacy to continue advancing the field's practices away from restrictive procedures.

Abstract

Abstract Behavior analysis is facing criticism based on its historical use of restrictive procedures and reliance on punishers to change behavior. A review of punishment procedures in behavior analytic literature has not been conducted since 2014; a more recent review can clarify whether the aforementioned criticism is substantiated by current research. In this review, we examined 59 studies published between 2014 and 2023 and extracted data on participant characteristics, procedural characteristics, and efficacy of procedures. We found an increase in the use of response interruption and redirection procedures, as well as more frequent evaluation of social validity, generalization, and maintenance as compared to previous decades. We also observed a decrease in functional assessment of target behaviors. Most importantly, we observed a decrease in physical forms of punishment, which may be considered more restrictive. We provide recommendations for future research, clinical practice, and advocacy.
