Summary

# Summary: Navigating Transition From IBI to Public School This qualitative study aimed to explore Board Certified Behavior Analysts' (BCBAs') experiences, challenges, and recommendations regarding the transition of autistic children from Intensive Behavioral Intervention (IBI) to public school settings. The researchers conducted focus groups with nine BCBAs to examine facilitators, barriers, current practices, and potential improvements in BCBA-school partnerships during this transition. Key findings indicated that relationship-building and rapport were identified as essential for successful transitions; however, BCBAs reported that limited collaboration with schools, knowledge gaps, and compensation issues frequently created obstacles to effective transition support. The study provides practical tools and recommendations for BCBAs to implement best practices and strengthen school partnerships, with implications for improving transition planning, coordination, and support systems for autistic learners and their families. The findings highlight the need for future research and policy development to standardize and enhance transition support practices across BCBA-school collaborations.

Abstract

The partnership between Board Certified Behavior Analysts (BCBAs) and schools in transitioning autistic (We intentionally use both identity-first (e.g., “autistic child”) and person-first language (e.g., “child with autism”) to respect the diverse language preferences of autistic individuals, caregivers, and professionals.) children from Intensive Behavioral Intervention (IBI) to public school is not well-documented. This qualitative study used focus groups to explore the experiences of nine BCBAs with this transition, examining facilitators, barriers, current practices, and recommendations for improvement. BCBAs described relationship-building and rapport as essential for successful transitions but noted that limited collaboration, knowledge gaps, and compensation issues often hindered their efforts. To address these barriers, this study provides practical tools to help BCBAs implement best practices and strengthen partnerships with schools.
