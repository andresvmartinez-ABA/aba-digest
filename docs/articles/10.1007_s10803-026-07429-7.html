Adaptive Educational Technologies for Students with Autism Spectrum Disorder: A Meta-analysis of Emotional and Social Engagement Outcomes.

Sofia Mastrokoukou, Angelos Chalkiadakis, Xanthippi Dima, Damaris Anyango Muga, Erastus Manoa Karani, C. Longobardi  ·  Journal of Autism and Developmental Disorders  ·  2026
TECHNOLOGY IN ABA
72/100Highly relevant meta-analysis examining adaptive educational technologies (VR/AR, tablet-based tools) for school-age children with ASD focusing on emotional and social engagement outcomes, directly aligned with stated interests in technology and autism interventions for preschool and school-age populations.
Summary

# Summary: Adaptive Educational Technologies for Students with ASD This meta-analysis examined whether adaptive educational technologies improve emotional engagement outcomes in students with autism spectrum disorder. The researchers analyzed 14 independent studies (2011–2024) involving 524 participants aged 4–17 years using random-effects modeling with restricted maximum-likelihood estimation to calculate pooled effect sizes. The overall pooled effect size was Hedges' g = 0.47 (95% CI 0.36–0.58), indicating a moderate positive effect of adaptive technologies on emotional engagement, with VR/AR-based tools showing the largest effects (g = 0.53); however, significant heterogeneity (I² = 69.22%) was observed, and studies with high risk of bias reported larger effect sizes than low-risk studies. BCBAs and autism practitioners should consider adaptive technologies as a potentially beneficial supplementary tool for supporting emotional engagement, while recognizing that intervention type (particularly VR/AR platforms) may influence effectiveness and that further research using consistent methodologies and validated outcome measures is needed. The findings suggest that while adaptive technologies show promise, variability in study quality and design warrants cautious interpretation and individualized consideration when implementing these tools in practice.

Abstract

Abstract Purpose Adaptive educational technologies are increasingly used to support emotional engagement in students with autism spectrum disorder (ASD), yet their overall effectiveness remains uncertain. This meta-analysis aimed to estimate the impact of adaptive technologies on emotional engagement outcomes and to examine methodological and study-level moderators. Methods Fourteen independent samples from 14 studies published between 2011 and 2024 were included, comprising 524 participants aged 4–17 years ( M age = 10.6, SD = 2.9; 21.7% female). Results Random-effects models with restricted maximum-likelihood (REML) estimation were used to calculate pooled effect sizes. The overall pooled effect size was Hedges’ g = 0.47 (95% CI 0.36–0.58), reflecting a moderate positive effect of adaptive technologies on emotional engagement. Significant heterogeneity was observed (I 2 = 69.22%), and moderator analyses indicated that intervention type (e.g., VR/AR vs. gamified or tablet-based platforms) significantly influenced outcomes ( p = .049). Effect sizes were highest for VR/AR-based tools ( g = 0.53). No significant differences emerged by age, geographical region, or study design. Studies with high risk of bias reported larger effect sizes than low-risk studies. Conclusions Overall, findings support the potential of adaptive technologies to improve emotional engagement in students with ASD. Methodological consistency and the use of validated outcome measures are recommended to enhance future evidence synthesis.
