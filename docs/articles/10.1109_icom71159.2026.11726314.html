Multimodal Assessment of Engagement: Evaluating the Engagement Metrics in Emotional Training Therapy

Summary

# Summary: Multimodal Assessment of Engagement in Robot-Assisted ASD Therapy The study investigated the relationships among multiple engagement metrics (task performance, robot engagement, physiological arousal, and gaze duration) during robot-assisted interventions for children with ASD and typically developing peers. The researchers conducted a correlational analysis with 60 children (30 with ASD, 30 typically developing) who interacted with QTrobot, measuring engagement through a multimodal framework that combined behavioral, perceptual, physiological, and observational data. Key findings included a significant positive correlation between robot engagement and task performance (r = 0.387, p = 0.020) and between robot gaze duration and robot engagement (r = 0.496, p = 0.010), while physiological arousal showed no significant correlation with either robot engagement or gaze duration. For BCBAs and autism practitioners, these results suggest that observable behavioral indicators of engagement—particularly gaze duration—may be more reliable markers of therapeutic progress than physiological measures, and that designing robot-assisted interventions to maximize behavioral engagement may lead to improved intervention outcomes. Robot developers and practitioners should consider prioritizing features that enhance behavioral engagement rather than focusing on physiological arousal as a primary engagement metric.

Abstract

While the use of socially assistive robots in Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) therapy is gaining prominence, the relationships among engagement metrics remain underexplored. This study aims to investigate the relationships between multimodal engagement metrics and their association with therapeutic task performance during robot-assisted interventions for children with ASD and typically developing (TD) children. Sixty children (30 typically developing, 30 children with ASD) participated in interaction with QTrobot. Engagement was assessed using a multimodal framework comprising task performance, robot engagement, physiological arousal, and robot gaze duration. Correlation analysis revealed a statistically significant positive relationship between robot engagement and task performance (r = 0.387, p = 0.020), suggesting that higher perceived engagement is associated to better intervention outcomes. A significant positive correlation was also found between robot gaze duration and robot engagement (r = 0.496, p = 0.010), indicating that gaze duration may serve as a behavioral indicator of engagement. In contrast, physiological arousal was not significantly correlated with either robot engagement (p = 0.782) or gaze duration (p = 0.446). These findings suggest that while gaze duration and perceived robot engagement are positively associated, therapeutic performance is more strongly associated with participants' engagement than with physiological arousal. Robot designs for ASD interventions should prioritize features that enhance behavioral engagement to improve therapeutic outcomes.
