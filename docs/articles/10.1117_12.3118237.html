Abstract

Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) affects approximately 1 in 31 children in the United States, presenting persistent challenges in social communication, behavioral regulation, and adaptive functioning. While evidence-based interventions such as Applied Behavior Analysis and cognitive-behavioral therapy have demonstrated efficacy, systemic barriers-including cost, limited accessibility, and low engagement-constrain their reach. Serious games offer a compelling alternative by leveraging children's affinity for digital play to create immersive, low-stakes environments for practicing complex skills. This paper presents the design, development, and preliminary evaluation of a serious 3D game intended to support the acquisition of social and behavioral skills in children with ASD. The game simulates real-world social scenarios such as turn-taking, emotion recognition, and nonverbal communication through interactive 3D environments. The game aimed to create a safe and controlled learning space where children can gradually build emotional resilience and social skills before transferring these abilities to real-world school settings. By combining immersive technology, guided scenarios, and emotional support mechanisms, the system seeks to enhance confidence, reduce anxiety, and improve school adaptation. Player experience was assessed using the Player Experience Inventory (PXI), and behavioral outcomes were measured using validated instruments. Early findings suggest the game supports meaningful skill development while sustaining high levels of player motivation and engagement. This work contributes to the growing body of research on technology-mediated interventions for ASD and offers a scalable, accessible complement to traditional therapeutic approaches.
