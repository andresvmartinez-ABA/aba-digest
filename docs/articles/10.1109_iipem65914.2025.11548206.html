# Summary: Cognitive Socially Assistive Robot for Multimodal Assessment and Adaptive Support in Autism Therapy The primary objective was to develop and describe a Cognitive Socially Assistive Mobile Robot (C-SAR) designed to improve therapeutic outcomes for children with ASD by providing multimodal assessment and real-time adaptive intervention for social communication deficits, joint attention difficulties, and emotional dysregulation. The C-SAR system, built on a TurtleBot3 platform, incorporates eye gaze tracking, facial expression recognition, and speech analysis to measure three behavioral metrics (Joint Attention Score, Social Engagement Score, and Speech Clarity Score) while delivering adaptive, game-like tasks with audiovisual feedback and autonomous prompt adjustment based on real-time behavioral cues. Preliminary validation results demonstrated 78–85% speech imitation accuracy, 85–90% gaze fixation success rates, and notably increased willingness to vocalize during interactive tasks compared to passive listening conditions. For BCBA practitioners, this work presents a mobile, clinically interpretable robotic platform that could augment traditional therapy by providing continuous, data-informed assessment and individualized intervention for children with ASD, while a therapist-facing dashboard supports longitudinal progress monitoring.

Abstract

Intervention for social communication deficits, joint attention difficulties, and emotional dysregulation in Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) requires constant, individualized, and data-informed intervention. This is typically an area where traditional clinical models may fail. This paper aims to describe a Cognitive Socially Assistive Mobile Robot (C-SAR), that focuses on improving therapeutic outcomes, through multimodal assessment and interactive engagement. The system- C-SAR, is built on a TurtleBot3 platform, and includes eye gaze tracking analysis, facial expression recognition analysis, and speech analysis, allowing it to calculate three key metrics of behavior, Joint Attention Score (JAS), Social Engagement Score (SES) and Speech Clarity Score (SCS). During intervention with the robot, children with ASD engage in adaptive, game-like tasks using real-time audiovisual feedback, with the robot providing positive reinforcement to strengthen attention, imitation, and social reciprocity. Leveraging on-device deep learning inference, the robot autonomously adapts prompts based on real-time behavioral cues while a therapist-facing dashboard provides longitudinal visualization. Preliminary validation demonstrates 78-85% speech imitation accuracy, 85-90% gaze fixation success rates, and notably increased willingness to vocalize compared to passive listening tasks. This work contributes a mobile, emotionally engaging, and clinically interpretable robotic platform that augments traditional therapy for children with ASD.
