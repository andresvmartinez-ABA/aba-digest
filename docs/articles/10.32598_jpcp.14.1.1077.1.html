Summary

# Summary: Virtual Reality to Improve Joint Attention Skills in Children With Autism The study aims to evaluate whether a virtual reality (VR)-based intervention can improve joint attention and social-communication skills in school-aged children with autism spectrum disorder, addressing a significant early developmental marker in ASD. The researchers employed a randomized controlled trial design in which participants were assigned to either a VR therapy plus standard interventions group or a sham VR condition plus standard interventions group, conducted in naturalistic school settings. The primary outcome measured was joint attention performance, with secondary outcomes assessing broader social-communication abilities; the abstract does not specify effect sizes or detailed results, as this is a protocol paper describing planned methodology rather than completed findings. For BCBAs and autism practitioners, this trial could provide evidence on the feasibility and potential clinical utility of VR as a supplemental, technology-supported intervention that may reduce barriers such as cost and resource demands while offering individualized, engaging learning environments. The authors acknowledge that the relatively small sample size and short-term follow-up period represent limitations that will inform recommendations for subsequent larger and longer-term studies.

Abstract

Joint attention (JA) deficits are frequently one of the earliest indicators of autism spectrum disorder (ASD), making them a crucial focus for early interventions.Despite the importance of JA, existing interventions often face barriers such as high costs, limited adaptability, and resource demands.Virtual reality (VR) offers a novel, immersive, and flexible approach that may overcome these challenges by creating engaging and individualized environments for children with ASD.This protocol describes a randomized controlled trial evaluating a VRbased intervention designed to improve JA and related social-communication skills in schoolaged autistic children.Participants will receive either VR therapy plus standard interventions or a sham VR condition plus standard interventions.The primary outcome is JA performance, with secondary outcomes focusing on broader social communication abilities.By testing this innovative, technology-supported intervention in naturalistic school settings, the trial aims to provide high-quality evidence on the feasibility and potential impact of VR as a supplemental therapy for autism.Key limitations include the relatively small sample size and short-term follow-up, which will guide recommendations for larger, long-term studies.
