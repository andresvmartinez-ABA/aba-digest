Bee-Bot-Assisted Activities for Students With Autism: A Pilot Quasi-Experimental Study of Social, Communication, and Emotional Outcomes

Summary

# Summary: Bee-Bot-Assisted Activities for Students With Autism This pilot study investigated whether participation in Bee-Bot-based activities was associated with improvements in social interaction, social communication, and emotional response skills among students with autism in a school setting. The researchers used a quasi-experimental pre-test–post-test design with 22 students with ASD (11 per group), where the experimental group completed 10 sessions of robotic-mediated activities with Bee-Bot while the control group completed identical activities without the robotic component, with outcomes measured using the GARS-3 subscales. Baseline-adjusted analyses revealed statistically significant between-group differences favoring the experimental group for social interaction (p < .001) and emotional response (p = .002), though results for social communication were inconclusive (p = .057). The authors emphasize that causal conclusions cannot be drawn due to the small sample size, non-equivalent groups at baseline, and exploratory design, and they call for confirmation through larger, randomized controlled trials.

Abstract

This exploratory pilot study examined whether participation in a Bee-Bot-based activity programme was associated with differences in social interaction, social communication, and emotional response skills within a school context. A quantitative quasi-experimental pre-test–post-test design with a non-equivalent control group was employed. The sample, recruited using purposive non-probability sampling, consisted of 22 students with ASD, with 11 participants in each group. All participants attended 10 individual intervention sessions. The experimental group participated in a Robotic Intervention Programme using Bee-Bot as a mediating educational tool, whereas the control group completed the same activities without robotic mediation. The Social Interaction (SI), Social Communication (SC), and Emotional Response (ER) subscales of the Gilliam Autism Rating Scale, Third Edition (GARS-3), were used as the three primary outcomes. Baseline-adjusted analyses showed statistically significant between-group differences in SI (Holm-adjusted p < .001) and ER (Holm-adjusted p = .002), with more favourable post-intervention scores in the experimental group. By contrast, the evidence for SC was inconclusive (Holm-adjusted p = .057). Sensitivity analyses based on change scores and additional adjustment for age and ASD support level showed the same overall pattern. These findings provide preliminary evidence that participation in structured Bee-Bot activities may be associated with more favourable outcomes in social interaction and emotional response. However, given the small sample size, baseline differences between non-equivalent groups, and exploratory design, causal conclusions cannot be drawn, and the findings require confirmation in larger, randomised studies.
