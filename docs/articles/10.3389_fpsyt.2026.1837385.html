Beyond distress relief: the Anhedonic Subtype of nonsuicidal self-injury and the imperative for Positive Affect Treatment.

Li, Cheng-Han, Qian, Jing, Qian, Jing-Ze et al.  ·  Frontiers in psychiatry  ·  2026
AUTOMATIC NEGATIVE REINFORCEMENT
75/100This article directly addresses the BCBA's interest in automatically reinforced self-injurious behavior and automatic negative reinforcement, while proposing a clinically applicable framework for understanding and treating a specific subtype of NSSI, though it focuses on adolescents rather than the preferred preschool/school-age populations.
Summary

This perspective article examines the traditional conceptualization of nonsuicidal self-injury (NSSI) and proposes recognition of a distinct "Anhedonic Subtype" that may require different treatment approaches. The authors synthesized epidemiological data highlighting "emptiness" as a central symptom and neurobiological evidence of reward system dysfunction to argue that some adolescents engage in NSSI not to reduce negative emotions, but as a "forced activation" mechanism to counteract profound anhedonia. The article proposes a preliminary diagnostic framework to distinguish between defensive dissociation and anhedonic deficit presentations of NSSI. The authors suggest that treatment for the anhedonic subtype should shift focus from traditional distress tolerance interventions toward positive affect enhancement strategies, though they acknowledge this framework requires future empirical validation.
