Summary

# Summary: Behavior Correction of Special Needs Students in Primary School The primary research question examined whether a personalized, multi-dimensional behavior correction intervention could effectively reduce problem behaviors in a third-grade student with autism spectrum disorder (ASD). The study employed a closed-loop model combining precise assessment, personalized intervention, and multi-party collaboration, utilizing literature review, observation, interviews, and action research methods to analyze the student's classroom problem behaviors and implement a comprehensive plan including positive reinforcement, environmental adaptation, and social skills training. Key findings demonstrated that after one semester of intervention, the participant's classroom aggressive behaviors decreased by 82%, stereotyped behaviors decreased by 75%, and social interaction initiative significantly improved. The results suggest that individualized intervention plans addressing student-specific differences, when combined with coordinated support from home, school, and community stakeholders, can effectively reduce problem behaviors in special needs students. These findings provide a practical model for behavior intervention implementation in primary school settings serving students with special needs.

Abstract

This study takes Xiaoyu, a third-grade primary school student with Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD), as the research object. Based on the closed-loop model of “precise assessment - personalized intervention - multi-party collaboration”, it systematically analyzes the manifestations and causes of his classroom problem behaviors through literature research, observation, interviews, and action research methods. A multi-dimensional behavior correction plan including positive reinforcement, environmental adaptation, and social skills training was formulated and implemented. After a semester of intervention, the frequency of Xiaoyu’s classroom aggressive behaviors decreased by 82%, the duration of stereotyped behaviors reduced by 75%, and his initiative in social interaction significantly improved. The research shows that personalized intervention plans designed for the individual differences of special needs students, combined with multi-party collaborative support from home, school, and society, can effectively correct their problem behaviors, providing reference for the practice of behavior intervention for special needs students in primary schools.
