Acoustic Markers of Engagement in Children with Autism Spectrum Disorder and Their Caregivers

Young-Suh Hong, Kaleen Shrestha, Austin Joel Dympep, M. Matarić  ·  2026

Summary

# Summary: Acoustic Markers of Engagement in Children with ASD and Their Caregivers This study aimed to identify acoustic features of child and caregiver vocalizations that distinguish engagement from disengagement during home-based socially assistive robot (SAR) interventions with children with ASD. The researchers analyzed vocalization patterns from home-based HCI interactions using linear mixed-effects models to examine acoustic features in both children and caregivers during engagement and disengagement states. The findings revealed three shared acoustic markers of engagement across both groups: mean Alpha Ratio, mean Harmonics-to-Noise Ratio, and mean Spectral Slope 500-1500 (all indicating cleaner and smoother vocalizations), and demonstrated that children exhibited significantly greater state-related acoustic variation than caregivers across these same three features. BCBAs and autism practitioners can use these acoustic markers as objective, automated indicators of engagement quality during technology-based or caregiver-facilitated interventions, potentially enabling real-time monitoring and adjustment of interaction dynamics to maintain or improve engagement.

Abstract

Characterizing user engagement is essential for developing effective human-computer interaction (HCI) applications, particularly for children with autism spectrum disorder (ASD). Because home-based HCI naturally involves caregivers as conversational facilitators, analyzing both child and caregiver vocalizations can provide critical insights into characterizing interaction dynamics. Using a dataset of home-based socially assistive robot (SAR) interventions, this work demonstrates how child and caregiver vocalization patterns can model engagement in HCI contexts. We employed linear mixed-effects models (LMMs) with child and caregiver acoustic data to identify acoustic features that significantly differentiated engagement from disengagement. The results demonstrated that children and caregivers share three significant acoustic markers of engagement: mean Alpha Ratio (AlphaRatio), mean Harmonics-to-Noise Ratio (HNR), and mean Spectral Slope 500-1500 (SlopeV500-1500). This reflects cleaner and smoother vocalizations during engagement across both groups. Furthermore, we evaluated a State x Group interaction model to compare the magnitudes of standardized state-dependent differences between children and caregivers. The results indicated that children exhibit significantly greater state-related variation than caregivers in three acoustic features: AlphaRatio, HNR, and SlopeV500-1500.
