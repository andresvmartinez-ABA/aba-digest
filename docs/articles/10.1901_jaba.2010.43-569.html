An investigation of differential reinforcement of alternative behavior without extinction.

Athens, Elizabeth S, Vollmer, Timothy R  ·  Journal of applied behavior analysis  ·  2010
COMPARING DDTTs (Delay to Reinforcement Tolerance Treatments)
85/100This article directly addresses problem behavior treatment using differential reinforcement strategies without extinction, which aligns closely with the profile's interests in severe problem behavior interventions, reinforcement science including motivating operations and reinforcer assessment, and functional analysis methodology.
Summary

This study investigated whether problem behavior could be reduced and appropriate behavior increased using differential reinforcement of alternative behavior (DRA) without implementing extinction procedures. The researchers conducted four experiments with seven children with developmental disabilities, systematically manipulating different dimensions of reinforcement (duration, quality, and delay) to favor appropriate behavior while still providing some reinforcement for problem behavior. Results from the first three experiments showed that behavior was sometimes sensitive to individual manipulations of reinforcement dimensions, but the most substantial and consistent improvements occurred in Experiment 4 when multiple reinforcement dimensions were combined to favor appropriate behavior. The findings suggest that DRA without extinction can be effective for behavior change when reinforcement parameters are strategically arranged to favor appropriate responses.
