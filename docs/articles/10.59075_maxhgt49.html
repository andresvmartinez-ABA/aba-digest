Speech-Language Pathologists’ Perspectives on the Impact of AI-Based Communication Applications on Expressive and Receptive Language Skills in Children with Autism Spectrum Disorder

Rizwan Khalil, S. Nawaz, Mahrukh Ramzan, Hajra Bibi  ·  The Critical Review of Social Sciences Studies  ·  2026

Summary

# Research Summary The study examined Speech-Language Pathologists' (SLPs') perspectives on how AI-based communication applications affect expressive and receptive language development in children with autism spectrum disorder. The researchers used a quantitative cross-sectional survey design, collecting data from 137 SLPs in Punjab and Islamabad through a self-developed Likert-scale questionnaire administered via non-probability purposive sampling. SLPs reported positive perceptions of AI applications for improving expressive language skills (vocabulary, requesting, communication initiation, and independent language use) and receptive language skills (vocabulary understanding through multimodal inputs, following instructions, and comprehension), with greatest perceived utility when AI tools were integrated alongside therapist-led intervention. Identified barriers to implementation included insufficient professional training, cost, limited internet connectivity, privacy concerns, and lack of support for Urdu and regional languages. BCBAs and autism practitioners should consider AI-based communication applications as supplementary tools within a comprehensive, therapist-led intervention framework rather than as standalone interventions, while addressing organizational and systemic barriers related to training, accessibility, and culturally appropriate language support.

Abstract

This study investigated Speech-Language Pathologists’ perspectives on the impact of AI-based communication applications on expressive and receptive language skills in children with Autism Spectrum Disorder. A quantitative descriptive cross-sectional survey design was used, and 137 Speech-Language Pathologists were selected through a non-probability purposive sampling technique from Punjab and Islamabad. Data were collected through a self-developed Likert-scale questionnaire. The findings revealed that SLPs perceived AI-based communication applications as useful supportive tools for improving expressive language skills, particularly vocabulary development, requesting, communication initiation, and independent language use. Respondents also reported positive perceptions regarding receptive language skills, especially understanding vocabulary through pictures and sounds, following simple instructions, and language comprehension. AI applications were considered clinically useful when used alongside therapist-led intervention. However, major barriers included lack of professional training, high cost, limited internet access, privacy concerns, and limited Urdu and regional language support. The study concluded that AI applications can support, but not replace, Speech-Language Pathologists.
