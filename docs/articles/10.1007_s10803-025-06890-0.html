Setting the Stage for an App-Based Caregiver-Mediated Intervention for Autism: Findings from A Stakeholder Consultation.

Monica Vanoncini, M. Cattaneo, Gloria Falcone, Mariachiara Fioravanti, Giulia de Leonardis, Laura Zampini et al.  ·  Journal of Autism and Developmental Disorders  ·  2025
TECHNOLOGY IN ABA
72/100Directly relevant as it addresses mobile/tablet-based teaching tools for autism, caregiver-mediated interventions, and technology-assisted behavioral support with strong stakeholder/social validity focus, though lacks specific ABA methodology detail.
Summary

# Summary This study investigated stakeholder perspectives on the design and implementation of a mobile health (mHealth) app-based caregiver-mediated intervention for autism, grounded in the WHO Caregiver Skills Training program. The researchers conducted focus groups and interviews with 13 caregivers and 14 clinicians, analyzing responses thematically to identify stakeholder needs and expectations. Thematic analysis revealed four primary themes: "Usage scenarios," "Purposes," "Threats to app success," and "Strategies for app success," with stakeholders emphasizing the need for an app supporting caregivers at various stages, integrating psychoeducation and home practice support, while identifying self-directed delivery (without therapist support) as a primary threat to effectiveness—mitigated through engaging content, gamification, customization, and ease-of-use features. The findings will inform the development of an app prototype based on caregiver needs and clinician recommendations, with future evaluation involving formal usability testing and assessment of the app's acceptability and effectiveness. BCBAs and autism practitioners should recognize that stakeholder input during mHealth tool development is critical for addressing real-world implementation barriers and may improve the likelihood of clinical adoption and caregiver engagement in technology-supported interventions.

Abstract

PURPOSE: The use of mobile technology to support clinical practice (mHealth) has the potential to support access to caregiver-mediated interventions for autistic children, improving accessibility, scalability, and cost-effectiveness. However, rarely stakeholders' perspectives have been integrated in the development of mHealth tools. This study aims to investigate stakeholders' needs, attitudes, and expectations regarding a to-be-developed mHealth caregiver-mediated intervention based on the WHO Caregiver Skills Training program. METHODS: We conducted focus groups and interviews with 13 caregivers and 14 clinicians. RESULTS: Thematic analysis identified four main themes: "Usage scenarios", "Purposes", "Threats to app success", and "Strategies for app success". Stakeholders emphasized the need for an app that could support caregivers at different stages and roles, regardless of previous exposure to in-person CST. Stakeholders expected an app that could integrate psychoeducation, support home practice, and promote psychological wellbeing. Perceived threats to the app's effectiveness pertained to the self-directed delivery, in absence of a therapist; while strategies proposed to counter such threats included incorporating engaging content, gamification and customization features, and overall ease of use of the app. CONCLUSION: Findings are discussed in relation to the development of a prototype based on caregivers' needs and recommendations from expert clinicians. Future directions involve conducting formal usability testing of the initial prototype and evaluating the app's acceptability and effectiveness.
