The Use of Contingency-Based Procedures to Promote Tolerance of Schedule Thinning for Hypothesized Multiply Maintained Problem Behavior.

Karla A. Zabala, Kara L. Wunderlich  ·  Behavior analysis in practice  ·  2022
PFA & FCT
95/100Manually curated paper from Research Rabbit library.
Summary

This study examined whether contingency-based procedures could increase tolerance to schedule thinning for problem behavior hypothesized to be maintained by multiple functions. The researchers implemented a contingency-based intervention with two participants and evaluated its effectiveness in promoting tolerance to delays in reinforcement. The results indicated that the contingency-based procedure was effective at facilitating tolerance for delays in both participants following the introduction of a lower effort task. The findings suggest that contingency-based procedures may be useful for addressing multiply maintained problem behavior in the context of schedule thinning.

Abstract

Contingency-based procedures have been found to be effective in facilitating delays to reinforcement for escape-maintained behavior within research and clinical contexts. Few studies have evaluated the use of these procedures for multiply maintained problem behavior. A contingency-based procedure was conducted with two participants to evaluate the effectiveness this procedure had on increasing tolerance to schedule thinning for problem behavior that was hypothesized to be multiply controlled. Results suggested that the procedure was effective at facilitating tolerance for a delay for both participants after switching to a lower effort task.
