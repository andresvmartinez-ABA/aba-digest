Oppositional Defiant Disorder in Autism and ADHD.

Mayes, Susan D, Pardej, Sara K, Waschbusch, Daniel A  ·  Journal of autism and developmental disorders  ·  2025
ASD + ADHD
75/100This article directly addresses comorbidity between autism and ADHD with behavioral manifestations (ODD), aligns with the profile's interest in ADHD-related reinforcement contingencies and ASD comorbidity, focuses on child populations, and has clear clinical implications for assessment and intervention despite not being purely ABA-focused.
Summary

This study examined the prevalence and characteristics of oppositional defiant disorder (ODD) across different diagnostic groups, comparing children with autism, ADHD-Combined presentation, and ADHD-Inattentive presentation. Mothers of 2,400 children aged 3-17 years completed the Pediatric Behavior Scale to assess ODD symptoms across various diagnostic combinations. The findings revealed that ADHD-Combined presentation was the strongest predictor of ODD, with prevalence rates of 53% in ADHD-Combined only and 62% when combined with autism, compared to lower rates in autism-only (24%) and ADHD-Inattentive groups (14-28%). Children with autism and ADHD-Combined showed significantly higher scores on the irritable/angry component of ODD compared to those with ADHD-Combined alone, while the oppositional/defiant component remained similar between groups. The researchers concluded that autism serves as an independent risk factor for ODD, particularly for irritable/angry behaviors, but the high co-occurrence of ADHD-Combined in autism (80%) largely explains the elevated ODD prevalence in autistic children.
