How Can Artificial Intelligence Adapt Virtual Reality Environments to the Individual Psychological, Sensory, and Cognitive Characteristics of Children with Neurodevelopmental Differences?

Yuliia Koshova  ·  European Journal of Contemporary Education and E-Learning  ·  2026
TECHNOLOGY IN ABA
75/100Highly relevant as it directly addresses AI-assisted VR interventions for children with autism and ADHD, focusing on individualized adaptations and practical intervention outcomes, though it lacks specific behavioral assessment/treatment depth and focuses more broadly on learning and sensory adaptation rather than specific ABA applications.
Summary

# Summary: AI-Adapted Virtual Reality for Children with Neurodevelopmental Differences This study investigated how artificial intelligence can customize virtual reality environments to match individual psychological, sensory, and cognitive characteristics of children with autism and ADHD, addressing the limitation that current VR tools target average users. The researchers conducted a systematic analysis of 12 adaptive VR systems (290 participants) and 14 intervention studies (279 participants), supplemented by the author's clinical practice records of 48 children, using exact statistical tests to examine adaptation features and adverse effects. Key findings indicated that all 12 systems adapted difficulty or feedback, only two adapted pacing or sensory parameters, learning-based logic appeared in more recent systems (median 2023) than fixed-rule systems (median 2016), and 70.8% of children required individualized adaptation configurations different from their group's typical settings; adverse effects were reported in six intervention studies with physical symptoms occurring only with immersive displays. The author proposes the PSC adaptation model, which prioritizes sensory tolerance before emotional regulation and cognitive challenge, suggesting that BCBAs and practitioners should consider individualized sensory parameters and adaptive algorithms rather than standardized VR protocols when implementing these tools. These findings indicate potential utility for AI-adapted VR in intervention planning, though practitioners should remain alert to possible adverse effects and the need for personalized configuration beyond group-level recommendations.

Abstract

Identification of autism and attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder among U.S. children keeps rising, while virtual reality (VR) tools for children with neurodevelopmental differences target an average user. This study tests how artificial intelligence (AI) adapts VR to a child's psychological, sensory and cognitive characteristics. Study-level data on 12 adaptive systems (290 participants) and 14 intervention studies (279 participants) were analyzed with exact tests, together with the author's practice records. All 12 systems adapted difficulty or feedback, and two adapted pacing or sensory parameters (p = 0.002). Learning-based logic appeared in later systems than fixed rules (median year 2023 against 2016, p = 0.006). Adverse effects were reported in six intervention studies; physical symptoms occurred only with immersive displays (p = 0.19). Forty-eight children in the practice records required 14 adaptation configurations; 70.8% differed from their group's modal configuration. The author proposes the PSC adaptation model, which ranks sensory tolerance before emotional regulation and cognitive challenge. The findings will interest psychologists, special educators, developers of immersive learning tools and researchers of child development.
