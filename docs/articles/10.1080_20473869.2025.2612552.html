The use of augmented reality to improve toilet training programs for children with autism spectrum disorder

Summary

# Summary: Augmented Reality for Toilet Training in Children with ASD The primary objective of this study was to develop an Augmented Reality (AR)-based learning tool to help children with Autism Spectrum Disorder understand and acquire toilet training skills through visual and interactive instruction. The researchers followed the ADDIE instructional design model and developed AR software using Unity, Blender, Vuforia Engine, and CorelDRAW, then evaluated its validity and practicality with content experts, media experts, and teachers before implementation. The study employed a one-group pretest-posttest design with nine children with ASD aged 7–10 years, and the Wilcoxon Signed-Rank test demonstrated statistically significant improvements in toilet training skills (Z = −2.666; p = 0.008); additionally, content expert validity was 89.6%, media expert validity was 92.5%, and teacher practicality ratings were 92.6%. The findings suggest that AR-based media may be an effective supplementary tool for teaching toilet training to children with ASD, particularly given its strong validity ratings and potential to address communication and visual processing challenges that limit conventional approaches. BCBAs and autism practitioners might consider AR-based instructional media as a potential aid for toilet training programs, though additional research with larger sample sizes would be needed to establish broader clinical recommendations.

Abstract

Toilet training is a fundamental skill crucial for fostering independence in children, including those with Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD). However, conventional approaches to toilet training are often ineffective due to communication limitations experienced by children with ASD. This study aims to develop an Augmented Reality (AR)-based learning media to help children with ASD understand the stages of toilet training visually and interactively. The development followed the ADDIE model and utilized software such as Unity, Blender, Vuforia Engine, and CorelDRAW. Validation results indicated a very high level of media feasibility, with content expert validity reaching 89.6% and media expert validity at 92.5%. Practicality assessment by teachers also showed high results, at 92.6%, indicating that the media is considered highly practical for use in learning. The media was tested on nine children with ASD aged 7–10 years using a one-group pretest-posttest design. The Wilcoxon Signed-Rank test showed a statistically significant improvement in toilet training skills (Z = −2.666; p = 0.008), leading to the conclusion that the use of AR media is effective in supporting toilet training programs for children with ASD.
