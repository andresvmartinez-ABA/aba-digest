Research on Scoring Mechanism and Rehabilitation Application of Metaverse 3-D Virtual Reality Technology in Social Adaptation System for Children with Autism Spectrum Disorder

Xuan-Lin Li, S. Gorbachev, Ashish Mani  ·  2025 Seventh International Conference on Research in Computational Intelligence and Communication Networks (ICRCICN)  ·  2025
TECHNOLOGY IN ABA
72/100Directly relevant as it addresses VR/immersive technology interventions for social skills and communication in preschool-age children with ASD, though the small sample size and limited behavioral specificity slightly reduce relevance.
Summary

# Summary: Metaverse 3-D Virtual Reality for Social Adaptation in Children with ASD The study aimed to develop and evaluate the Metaverse Interactive Social Adaptation System (MISAS), a virtual reality intervention designed to improve social adaptation and emotional regulation in children with autism spectrum disorder. The researchers employed a quasi-experimental pre-test/post-test design with 8 children (ages 4–8 years, mean age 5.5) who received a 3-week intervention using MISAS, which incorporates 12 thematic scenarios and 31 tasks based on the Vineland Adaptive Behavior Scales, Second Edition (VABS-II). Paired-samples t-tests demonstrated statistically significant improvements in adaptive behavior scores, with a mean increase of 18.36 points (Cohen's d = 6.28, 95% CI [10.52, 26.20], p = 0.006), with domain-specific weighting across daily living skills (0.41), communication (0.25), socialization (0.21), and motor skills (0.12). The results suggest that immersive virtual reality technology may offer a promising approach to increase engagement and improve adaptive behavior outcomes in children with ASD, though BCBAs should note the small sample size and short intervention duration when considering implementation. Further research with larger samples and longer-term follow-up is needed to establish durability of skill gains and identify which child populations benefit most from this technology-based intervention.

Abstract

Children with autism spectrum disorders (ASD) face challenges in social adaptation and emotional regulation due to developmental deficits in psychological and social abilities. Uneven resource allocation and insufficient immersive experiences limit the effectiveness of traditional rehabilitation approaches, resulting in low engagement and poor skill transfer among children with autism. To address this issue, we developed the Metaverse Interactive Social Adaptation System (MISAS) for children with ASD. Based on Vineland Adaptive Behavior Scales, Second Edition (VABS-II), it includes 12 thematic scenarios and 31 tasks. Using a quasi-experimental pre-test/post-test design, 8 ASD children ($4-8$ years old, mean 5.5) received 3-week intervention. Paired-samples t-test showed significant score improvement (mean increase: 18.36, Cohen’s $\mathrm{d}=6.28$, 95% CI [10.52,26.20], $\mathrm{p}=0.006$). VABS-II domain weights (daily living skills: 0.41, communication: 0.25, socialization: 0.21, motor skills: $\mathbf{0. 1 2}$) were determined by normative standards and clinical practice.
