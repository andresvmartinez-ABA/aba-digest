THE SCHOOL TRANSITIONING EFFECTS OF VIRTUAL REALITY-BASED VIDEO MODELLING INTERVENTION ON AUTISTIC CHILDREN

Lin-Ang Yi, Sima Ahmadpour, H. Goh, Kang-Lee Chen, K. Pheh  ·  Malaysian Journal of Computer Science  ·  2026
TECHNOLOGY IN ABA
78/100Directly relevant pilot study on VR-based intervention for school transitions in preschool/early school-age autistic children targeting social communication and behavioral outcomes, aligning with interests in technology/autism, social skills, and ASD populations, though limited by small sample size and lack of functional analysis methodology.
Summary

This pilot feasibility study investigated whether a customizable Virtual Reality Video Modelling (VRVM) intervention could improve school-transition-related behavioral and social outcomes in autistic children prior to school enrollment. Seven autistic children aged 5–7 years participated in weekly VRVM training sessions from January to March 2024, using a Google Cardboard head-mounted display to view three point-of-view video modelling scenarios depicting school-related social situations, with outcomes measured via the Social Responsiveness Scale–Second Edition (SRS-2) and Behavior Assessment System for Children–Third Edition (BASC-3). Pre- to post-intervention comparisons yielded large effect sizes for hyperactivity (d = 1.247) and medium-to-large effects for the Behavioral Symptoms Index (d = 0.739), with medium effects observed for social awareness (d = 0.546); however, the authors acknowledged that effect sizes from small samples may be upwardly biased and should be interpreted cautiously. For BCBA practitioners, these preliminary findings suggest that VRVM may serve as a feasible, augmentative tool for preparing autistic children for school transitions by targeting relevant behavioral and social skills. The authors emphasize that larger, controlled research studies are necessary to confirm the efficacy of this combined intervention approach before widespread clinical implementation.

Abstract

Autistic children frequently experience significant difficulties during school transitions, including challenges with social communication, behavioral regulation, and adapting to unfamiliar environments. Although both Virtual Reality (VR) and Video Modelling (VM) have been independently investigated as effective interventions for Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD), their combined application specifically targeting school transition behaviors remains underexplored in the literature. This pilot pre-post feasibility study examined the effects of a customizable Virtual Reality Video Modelling (VRVM) intervention on school-transition-related behavioral and social outcomes in autistic children. Seven autistic children (aged 5–7 years) participated in weekly VRVM training sessions conducted from January to March 2024, prior to school enrolment. The VRVM application, delivered via a Google Cardboard head-mounted display (HMD) on an Android device, presented three point-of-view video modelling storylines depicting school-related social scenarios. Pre- and post-intervention assessments were conducted using the Social Responsiveness Scale - Second Edition (SRS-2) and the Behavior Assessment System for Children - Third Edition (BASC-3). Effect sizes were quantified using Cohen's d. Pre- and post-intervention scores showed improvements across multiple scales. The Cohen's d values were d = 1.247 for hyperactivity (large effect), d = 0.739 for Behavioral Symptoms Index from BASC-3 (medium-to-large effect), and d = 0.546 for social awareness from SRS-2 (medium effect). Hoever, effect sizes derived from small samples may be upwardly biased and should be interpreted with caution. Conclusions: These preliminary findings suggest that VRVM is a feasible and promising augmentative approach for supporting school transition in autistic children. Future controlled research with larger samples is warranted to confirm these effects.
