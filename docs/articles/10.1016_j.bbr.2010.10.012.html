Individual differences in vulnerability for self-injurious behavior: studies using an animal model.

Muehlmann, Amber M, Wilkinson, Jennifer A, Devine, Darragh P  ·  Behavioural brain research  ·  2011
SIB PREDICTORS
75/100This article directly addresses the BCBA's interest in the neurobiological underpinnings of self-injurious behavior and automatic negative reinforcement mechanisms, using an animal model to explore vulnerability factors that could inform clinical understanding and treatment approaches.
Summary

This study investigated the neurobiological mechanisms that make some individuals vulnerable to developing self-injurious behavior (SIB) while others remain resistant, using an animal model. Researchers screened 18 Long-Evans rats for their behavioral responsiveness to novel environment stress, categorized them as high responders (HR) or low responders (LR), then administered daily pemoline injections for 10 days to induce SIB. All HR rats and 5 of 9 LR rats exhibited self-injury, with HR rats demonstrating significantly more time spent self-injuring, more injured body sites, and larger areas of tissue damage compared to LR rats. The findings revealed that behavioral responsiveness to novelty stress was significantly correlated with all measures of self-injury, suggesting that innate stress responsiveness can predict vulnerability to developing SIB.
