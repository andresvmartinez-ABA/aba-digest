Digital Training Based on Artificial Intelligence to Improve Speech Skills among Individuals with Autism Spectrum Disorder: Perspectives of Rehabilitation Center Workers in Oman

Summary

# Summary: Digital AI-Based Training for Speech Skills in Autism (Oman) This study sought to explore how artificial intelligence-based digital training applications improve speech and language skills among children with ASD in Omani rehabilitation centers, with particular focus on practitioners' perspectives regarding digital infrastructure, program design, implementation, and outcomes. The research employed a descriptive-analytical design using an electronic questionnaire administered to 100 practitioners (speech therapists, special education teachers, behavioral specialists, and supervisors), with data analyzed through means, standard deviations, t-tests, ANOVA, correlation, and regression analyses. Key findings indicated moderate availability of the digital environment with significant barriers (software licensing and internet stability issues), moderate-level training design and implementation with gaps in individualization and generalization to real-world contexts, and improvements in speech (mean = 3.74) and speech fluency (mean = 3.57), though stuttering symptoms persisted. For BCBAs and autism practitioners, these results suggest that while AI-based digital training shows promise for speech improvement, implementation requires attention to infrastructure development, individualized program modifications, and structured transition strategies to support generalization beyond the digital environment and increase parental involvement in follow-up care.

Abstract

This study aims to explore the reality of digital training based on artificial intelligence applications in improving speech and language skills among children with Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) in rehabilitation centers in the Sultanate of Oman. It focuses on the perspectives of practitioners regarding the digital environment and facilities, program design, implementation and follow-up mechanisms, and their impact on speech and fluency development. The study adopted a descriptive-analytical methodology using an electronic questionnaire that included five main research questions: the status of the digital environment and facilities, the quality of training design, mechanisms of implementation and follow-up, the impact of training on speech improvement, and its effect on enhancing speech fluency. The sample consisted of 100 participants, including speech therapists, special education teachers, behavioral specialists, and supervisors. Data were analyzed using means, standard deviations, t-tests, ANOVA, as well as correlation and regression analyses. Results revealed that the digital environment was moderately available with notable challenges such as software licensing and internet stability. Training design and implementation were of a moderate level, with shortcomings in addressing individual differences, transitioning from digital to real-life contexts, and in follow-up with parental involvement. Speech improvement recorded the highest mean (3.74), while speech fluency achieved a relatively high mean (3.57), despite persisting stuttering symptoms. Overall, findings indicate a moderate effectiveness of digital training programs and highlight the need for further development.
