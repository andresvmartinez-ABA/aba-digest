Telehealth Versus In-Person Caregiver-Mediated Behavioral Treatment for Challenging Behaviors in Children With Autism Spectrum Disorder: Protocol for COACH (Caregiver Outreach for Autism Coaching at Home) Randomized Controlled Trial.

Pollard, Joy S, Quiroz, Leslie S, Boyd, Jennifer A et al.  ·  JMIR research protocols  ·  2026
RANDOM INTERESTS (PFA & FCT)
95/100This article directly aligns with multiple core interests including practical functional assessment, caregiver-mediated behavioral treatment for challenging behaviors in preschool/school-age children with autism, and applied research with clear clinical applications.
Summary

This study aims to compare the effectiveness of telehealth versus in-person delivery of caregiver-mediated behavioral treatment for challenging behaviors in children with autism spectrum disorder (ASD). The methodology involves a randomized controlled trial with 90 caregiver-child dyads (children aged 2-7 years) allocated to three groups: telehealth-delivered treatment, in-person treatment, or online psychoeducation, with interventions delivered weekly over 12 weeks following functional assessments. As this is a protocol paper describing an ongoing study, no findings are reported yet, with data collection expected to conclude in October 2026 and analysis planned for Winter 2026 through Spring 2027. The study addresses gaps in scalable, family-centered approaches to reduce challenging behaviors and aims to inform best practices for telehealth-delivered behavioral interventions.
