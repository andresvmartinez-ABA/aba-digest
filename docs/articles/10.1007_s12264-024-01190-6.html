Summary

# Summary: Extended Reality in Treating Children with Autism Spectrum Disorder The primary research objective was to compare Extended Reality (XR) theoretical models, application examples, and intervention effects for treating social interaction, communication difficulties, and repetitive behaviors in children with Autism Spectrum Disorder. The study employed a comparative review methodology examining XR intervention approaches grounded in cognitive rehabilitation, teaching, and social-emotional learning theories, incorporating technologies such as algorithms, artificial intelligence, eye-tracking, and multimodal interactive platforms. Participants demonstrated effective improvements in competency barriers related to social, communication, and self-regulation skills through XR-based interventions, though the abstract does not specify effect sizes or quantitative outcome measures. For BCBA practitioners, XR technology presents a potentially convenient supplemental intervention approach that addresses limitations of traditional extended treatment periods; however, further development of Mixed Reality technology and optimization of XR-AI multimodal interaction models are necessary before widespread clinical implementation. The authors recommend that future research prioritize developing MR intervention scenarios and maintaining optimal intervention levels to establish evidence-based practice standards for XR-assisted ASD treatment.

Abstract

Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) is a common neurodevelopmental disorder in children, characterized by social interaction, communication difficulties, and repetitive and stereotyped behaviors. Existing intervention methods have limitations, such as requiring long treatment periods and needing to be more convenient to implement. Extended Reality (XR) technology offers a virtual environment to enhance children's social, communication, and self-regulation skills. This paper compares XR theoretical models, application examples, and intervention effects. The study reveals that XR intervention therapy is mainly based on cognitive rehabilitation, teaching, and social-emotional learning theories. It utilizes algorithms, models, artificial intelligence (AI), eye-tracking, and other technologies for interaction, achieving diverse intervention outcomes. Participants showed effective improvement in competency barriers using XR-based multimodal interactive platforms. However, Mixed Reality (MR) technology still requires further development. Future research should explore multimsodal interaction technologies combining XR and AI, optimize models, prioritize the development of MR intervention scenarios, and sustain an optimal intervention level.
