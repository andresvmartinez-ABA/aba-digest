Emotional Regulation of Young Children with Autism Spectrum Disorders: A Systematic Review

SoHyun Lee, Eui-Jung An, Ralm Yun, Youngseon Chang, Healim Chung  ·  The Korean Association For Persons With Autism  ·  2022
95/100 Manually curated paper from Research Rabbit library.
Summary

This systematic review examined interventions designed to improve emotional regulation in young children with Autism Spectrum Disorders (ASD). The researchers analyzed 56 experimental studies to identify intervention types and their specific characteristics for supporting emotional regulation development. The most frequently used interventions were technology-assisted instruction/intervention (42.9% of studies), social skills training (37.5%), and cognitive behavior instruction strategies (33.9%). The dependent variable most commonly addressed across studies was "cognitive evaluation of emotions," which appeared in 69.6% of the reviewed research. The review identified 14 distinct intervention approaches used to target emotional regulation skills in this population.

Abstract

Young children with Autism Spectrum Disorders (ASD) often experience difficulties developing emotional regulation because of their unique characteristics; therefore, it is necessary to support them. This article reviews previous intervention studies related to emotional regulations of young children with ASD. According to the criteria, a total of 56 experimental studies were selected and the general characteristics and their specific contents for emotional regulation of young children with ASD were analyzed; in total, 14 interventions were used to improve emotional regulation. The most used interventions were technology assisted instruction/intervention (TAII; 24, 42.9%), social skills training (SST; 21, 37.5%) and cognitive behavior instruction strategies (CBIS; 19, 33.9%). Regarding dependent variables, ‘cognitive evaluation of emotions’ was covered in 39 studies (69.6%), showing the highest rate. Based on the results, implications and future directions to improve the emotional regulation ability of young children with ASD are discussed.
