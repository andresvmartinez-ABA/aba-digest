NeuroSense: A Real-Time Behaviour and Sensory Trigger Detection Framework for Children with Autism Using Multimodal Vision and Stimulus Feedback

Summary

# NeuroSense: Real-Time Behavior and Sensory Trigger Detection—Research Summary The primary objective was to develop and evaluate NeuroSense, a real-time application designed to detect behavioral triggers and sensory responses in children with ASD during classroom and therapy activities, while providing adaptive feedback to maintain engagement. The study employed a multimodal vision-based approach using MediaPipe to extract full-body pose dynamics, facial micro-expressions, and eye movement data from video recordings of three children with ASD across 42 sensory learning sessions, with a Random Forest classifier trained to distinguish behavioral responses from labeled trigger and non-trigger intervals. The system achieved 92.4% accuracy in offline classification of behavioral responses and demonstrated that real-time adaptive prompts (teacher voice cues, visual activities, or tactile suggestions) reduced direct teacher intervention by 67%, increased session completion rates from 58% to 89%, and enabled average engagement recovery within 14.2 seconds following attention breaks. For BCBA practitioners, NeuroSense represents a potential classroom support tool that combines automated behavior detection with real-time intervention delivery, potentially reducing reliance on constant teacher monitoring while increasing session productivity for learners with ASD. The non-intrusive, vision-based technology may be particularly valuable for practitioners seeking to implement data-driven, responsive feedback systems that support individualized learning experiences and reduce staff burden during structured learning activities.

Abstract

Children diagnosed with Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) often face difficulties in maintaining attention, understanding sensory inputs, and expressing behavioral reactions. This work presents NeuroSense, a lightweight support application that detects behavioral triggers and aids engagement during classroom and therapy activities. The system uses full-body pose dynamics, facial micro-expressions, and eye movement cues from video via MediaPipe, allowing for observation without intrusive sensors.Video recordings from three children with ASD were divided into labeled trigger and non-trigger intervals, as noted by teachers during 42 sensory learning sessions. A feature pipeline that included joint distances, motion statistics, and facial activity descriptions was created to train a Random Forest classifier, which achieved 92.4% accuracy in distinguishing behavioral responses. In addition to offline analysis, NeuroSense offers real-time attention monitoring. When the system notices a drop in engagement, it automatically presents personalized prompts, such as teacher voice cues, visual activities, or tactile suggestions. During its use, the app cut the need for direct teacher intervention by 67% and increased session completion rates from 58% to 89%, with an average recovery from attention breaks occurring within 14.2 seconds.These results show that NeuroSense can serve as an effective classroom support tool, combining behavior sensing, prediction, and adaptive feedback. The system demonstrates how accessible, vision-based technology can help educators and caregivers manage attention and support personalized learning experiences for autistic children.
