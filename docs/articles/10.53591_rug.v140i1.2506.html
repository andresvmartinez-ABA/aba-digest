Aplicación Terapéutica de la Realidad Virtual en Niños y Adolescentes con Trastorno del Espectro Autista

Fernando Mauricio León Martínez, Mateo Fabian Aguilar Apolo, Sebastian Andres Vasquéz Argudo, Denis Alexander Sáenz Benenaula  ·  Revista Universidad de Guayaquil  ·  2026
TECHNOLOGY IN ABA
72/100The article directly addresses virtual reality interventions for social skills in children and adolescents with autism (key interests), though it lacks specific behavioral mechanisms, functional analysis details, and appears to be a broad systematic review without deep clinical application specifics.
Summary

This systematic review evaluated the effectiveness of virtual reality (VR) as a therapeutic application for children and adolescents with Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) compared to traditional therapy approaches. The methodology involved analyzing studies published between 2020 and 2025 in English or Spanish that assessed VR interventions in individuals aged 3–18 years diagnosed with ASD using DSM-5 or ICD-11 criteria. The review found that virtual reality significantly improves social skills in children and adolescents with ASD and demonstrated high acceptance and adherence rates among participants. The authors identified methodological limitations across included studies and noted that greater standardization and long-term follow-up research are needed to establish robust evidence. For BCBAs and autism practitioners, these findings suggest that VR may serve as an effective supplemental tool for social skills intervention, though practitioners should await further standardized research and consider VR applications as part of a comprehensive, evidence-based treatment plan rather than as a standalone intervention.

Abstract

Objective: To evaluate the effectiveness of virtual reality as a therapeutic application in children and adolescents with Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) in comparison to traditional therapy. Materials and Methods: Studies published between 2020 and 2025, in English or Spanish, were included. These studies assessed the therapeutic use of virtual reality (VR) in children and adolescents diagnosed with Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD), aged between 3 and 18 years, based on recognized clinical criteria (DSM-5 or ICD-11). Results: The systematic review shows that virtual reality significantly improves social skills in children and adolescents with ASD. Despite its high acceptance and adherence, methodological limitations remain, and greater standardization and long term follow up are needed. Discussion: Different authors' points of view are compared to better understand the positive and negative aspects of this therapy. Conclusion: It is a highly effective tool, supported by several studies, demonstrating that it opens the door to improved care for these patients.
