Utilizing Human–Robot Interaction in Autism Therapy to Enhance Children’s Social Skills: Literature Review

Norah Alkhaldi, Maha Faisal, A. Mutawa  ·  ACM Trans. Hum. Robot Interact.  ·  2025

Summary

# Summary: Human–Robot Interaction in Autism Therapy The primary objective of this systematic review was to evaluate advancements in Robot-Assisted Autism Therapy (RAAT) from 2013 to 2023, specifically examining benefits and limitations, assessment metrics, and technological innovations for enhancing social skills in children with autism. Following PRISMA guidelines, the researchers screened 2,396 papers from IEEE Xplore and ACM Digital Library databases, ultimately including 46 peer-reviewed English-language studies focused on human–robot interaction and user experience in RAAT for children. Key findings indicated a preference for humanoid robots in therapy sessions, particularly for targeting imitation and joint attention development, and identified the need for user-friendly therapist interfaces and improved robot autonomy as critical design considerations. The review highlights that BCBAs and autism practitioners should consider integrating robot-assisted interventions as a supplementary tool for social skills development, while prioritizing accessible and ethical interaction design to enhance engagement. The authors recommend that future RAAT research prioritize artificial intelligence integration and development of comprehensive datasets to address current gaps in the field and improve clinical outcomes.

Abstract

Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) is a neurodevelopmental condition characterized by differences in social communication and interaction, highlighting the importance of early and supportive engagement. Robot-Assisted Autism Therapy (RAAT) has emerged as a promising approach that utilizes repetitive, interactive sessions to facilitate engagement and learning experiences. This systematic review, conducted following PRISMA guidelines, evaluates advancements in RAAT from 2013 to 2023, addressing three key questions: the benefits and limitations, the metrics used to assess success, and the technological advancements aimed at overcoming challenges. After screening 2,396 papers from IEEE Xplore and ACM Digital Library, 46 studies were included. These studies met the following inclusion criteria: English-language, peer-reviewed journal articles and conference proceedings focusing on RAAT advancements for children, specifically addressing human–robot interaction (HRI) and user experience (UX). The studies were categorized by demographics, methods, robot types, targeted behaviors, HRI modes, UX, and ethical considerations. Our findings highlight a preference for humanoid robots, particularly in sessions supporting imitation and joint attention development. The review also emphasizes the need for user-friendly therapist interfaces and addresses challenges related to robot autonomy. Future research should focus on integrating artificial intelligence and developing comprehensive datasets to fill current gaps in RAAT systems. Overall, this review identifies emerging trends, limitations, and provides guidance for advancing robot-assisted support for autistic children through accessible, engaging, and ethical interaction design.
