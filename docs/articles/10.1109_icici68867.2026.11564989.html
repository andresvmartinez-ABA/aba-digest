Summary

# Summary: Adaptive Affective-Aware AAC Storytelling Framework for Children with Autism Using Reinforcement Learning The primary objective was to develop an augmentative and alternative communication (AAC) system that generates emotionally responsive storytelling by adapting tone, complexity, and pacing based on real-time affective cues in children with autism. The study employed a reinforcement learning framework with affective computing, utilizing multiple emotion-specific Q-tables, prioritized experience replay, and adaptive learning rates to create personalized communication policies across 50 simulated interaction sessions. Results demonstrated a 52% increase in engagement, 36% gain in comprehension, and 30% reduction in stress compared to conventional AAC baselines, with statistical significance (p < 0.001, d ≈ 1.7). These findings suggest that AAC systems incorporating dynamic emotional responsiveness and personalized adaptation may enhance communication outcomes for children with autism during storytelling activities. However, the simulation-based methodology indicates that further clinical validation with actual end-users is needed before practical implementation in clinical or educational settings.

Abstract

Children with Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) often struggle to express and engage emotions in storytelling encounters. Little attention has been paid to the generation of intonation (emotion) except that nearly all current AAC systems produce output according to a rule-base without consideration of affective variation. This work proposes an Adaptive AAC (Augmentative and Alternative Communication) Storytelling Framework with Reinforcement Learning (RL) and Affective Computing for adaptive, emotive responsive communication. The RL agent dynamically adjusts story complexity, tone, and pacing in response to affective cues of engagement, stress, curiosity and attention. The hybrid meta-learned policy is achieved by using multiple emotion-specific Q-tables prioritized experience replay (PER) and adaptive learning rates to learn a personalized policy through fast convergence and adaptation for individual needs. Experimental results from 50 simulated interaction sessions showed a 52 % increase in engagement, a 36 % gain in comprehension and a 30% savings in stress with respect to conventional AAC baselines. These findings are supported by statistical tests (p < 0.001, d ≈ 1.7). Limitations and pathways to real-world clinical validation are discussed.
