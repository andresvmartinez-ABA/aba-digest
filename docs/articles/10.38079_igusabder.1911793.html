Evaluation of Artificial Intelligence-Based Early Intervention and Education Applications

Summary

This study aimed to evaluate AI-based early intervention and educational applications for children across parameters including target population suitability, technological infrastructure, advantages, limitations, and developmental appropriateness. The researchers conducted a qualitative expert-based screening by examining five applications (Duolingo ABC, Khanmigo, DreamBox, Suufle, and TADİS) through website and documentation review, with four child development experts achieving 86.11% consistency in their evaluations across seven main parameters. The findings showed that three applications (Duolingo ABC, Khanmigo, DreamBox) primarily supported academic and cognitive skills through adaptive mechanisms, while two applications (TADİS and Suufle) focused on language, communication, and social participation skills for children with autism spectrum disorder; however, all applications demonstrated limitations in technological transparency, cultural adaptation, data security, and independent scientific validation of effectiveness. BCBAs and autism practitioners should recognize that while AI applications offer potential benefits for personalized learning and social communication support, careful evaluation is needed regarding data security, ethical considerations, scientific evidence, and developmental appropriateness before implementation. The authors recommend that AI applications for children be developed and evaluated within a comprehensive framework integrating technological innovation with established pedagogical and developmental principles rather than through technological advancement alone.

Abstract

Aim:This study was conducted to evaluate artificial intelligence (AI)-based early intervention and educational applications developed for children from the perspective of Child Development experts, in line with the parameters of the target population, area of use, technological infrastructure, advantages, limitations, and developmental appropriateness.

Method:The study was designed as an expert-based qualitative screening study. Data were collected by examining the official websites, product promotion pages, user information pages, and privacy policies of the applications. A total of 17 early intervention and educational applications were identified through a web-based search; among these, five applications that met the predetermined criteria were included in the study. The applications reviewed were Duolingo ABC, Khanmigo, DreamBox, Suufle, and TADİS. The evaluation process was carried out by four researchers who are experts in child development, and the consistency rate among expert responses was 86.11%.

Results:The applications were evaluated across seven main parameters. The findings indicated that Duolingo ABC, Khanmigo, and DreamBox primarily supported academic skills, individualized learning, and cognitive development through adaptive mechanisms, whereas TADİS and Suufle contributed to the language, communication, and social participation skills of children with autism spectrum disorder. However, limitations were identified in certain applications regarding technological transparency, cultural adaptation, data security, and independent scientific evidence of effectiveness.

Conclusion:The results showed that AI-based early intervention and educational applications for children offer significant opportunities for personalized learning, motivation, accessibility, language development, and support for social communication skills. However, these applications should be carefully evaluated with regard to ethics, data security, scientific evidence, and developmental appropriateness. In this respect, it is recommended that AI applications developed for children be addressed not only through technological innovation but also within a holistic framework grounded in pedagogical and developmental principles.
