Summary

# Summary: The Impact of Visual Supports on Classroom Engagement of a Student with Autism This action research study investigated whether visual supports (visual schedules, pictorial task breakdowns, and transition timers) could improve classroom engagement in a Grade 5 student with autism in an inclusive UAE school setting. The intervention was implemented over six weeks, with data collected through structured observation checklists, Learning Support Assistant reflection journals, and semi-structured teacher interviews. Quantitative findings showed substantial improvements: task completion increased from 50% to 85%, transition success improved from 40% to 80%, and on-task engagement rose from 55% to 90%; qualitative data indicated reduced anxiety, increased predictability, and greater student independence. BCBAs and autism practitioners should consider visual supports as a cost-effective, adaptable intervention strategy and should prioritize training for Learning Support Assistants and teachers, embedding these practices into school policies, and establishing home-school collaboration to maintain consistency across settings.

Abstract

Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) is a neurodevelopmental condition characterized by persistent challenges in social communication, restricted interests, and repetitive behaviors. These characteristics often lead to barriers in inclusive educational environments, where unpredictability and reliance on verbal communication can increase anxiety and hinder engagement. As global initiatives emphasise inclusive education through international policy frameworks and Sustainable Development Goals, it becomes imperative to identify practical, evidence-based interventions that promote the successful participation of autistic students in mainstream classrooms. Visual supports—including visual schedules, task cards, and timers—are widely recognized as evidence-based practices that provide clarity, structure, and predictability. However, while research in Western contexts has consistently demonstrated their effectiveness, studies in Middle Eastern inclusive settings remain limited. This action research study investigates the impact of visual supports on classroom engagement of a Grade 5 student with autism in an inclusive school in the United Arab Emirates. The intervention lasted six weeks and included the introduction of daily visual schedules, step-by-step pictorial task breakdowns, and transition timers. Data were collected through structured observation checklists, Learning Support Assistant (LSA) reflection journals, and semi-structured teacher interviews. Quantitative data revealed improvements in task completion (from 50% to 85%), transition success (from 40% to 80%), and on-task engagement (from 55% to 90%). Qualitative findings highlighted reduced anxiety, increased predictability, greater independence, and improved teacher perceptions of participation. The study concludes that visual supports are a cost-effective, adaptable, and impactful strategy for inclusive classrooms. Implications include the need for training LSAs and teachers, embedding visual supports into school policies, and fostering home-school collaboration to ensure consistency. This research contributes context-specific evidence from the UAE, reinforcing global calls for inclusive practices that enable autistic students to thrive.
