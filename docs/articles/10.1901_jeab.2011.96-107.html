Reinforcement delay fading during differential reinforcement of communication: the effects of signals on response maintenance.

Kelley, Michael E, Lerman, Dorothea C, Fisher, Wayne W et al.  ·  Journal of the experimental analysis of behavior  ·  2011
COMPARING DDTTs (Delay to Reinforcement Tolerance Treatments)
85/100This article directly addresses differential reinforcement of communication and delay tolerance treatments with signaled reinforcement, which are core interests in the profile related to functional communication training and reinforcement science methodologies.
Summary

This study examined whether providing signals during reinforcement delays helps maintain communication responses when delays to reinforcement are gradually introduced in differential reinforcement procedures. Three individuals participated in experiments using multiple-schedule and withdrawal designs that compared signaled versus unsignaled reinforcement delays of increasing duration. Results for two of the three participants demonstrated that signals during delays facilitated better maintenance of communication responses compared to unsignaled delays. The findings suggest that signaling procedures may be effective for preserving responding when implementing delay fading in differential reinforcement of communication interventions.
