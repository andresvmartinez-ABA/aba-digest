Abstract

Socially assistive robots are increasingly used to support therapeutic and educational interventions for individuals with Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD). Their predictable behavior and simple social cues can promote engagement and communication during therapy sessions [13]–[15]. This paper presents the design and development of an upperbody humanoid robot for gesture-based assistive interaction. The robot performs basic social gestures, such as hand waving and head nodding, to encourage user engagement. Gesture motions were first validated in CoppeliaSim and then implemented on a physical prototype using 3D-printed components and servoactuated joints. To enhance interaction, a lightweight convolutional neural network (CNN) is integrated for facial emotion recognition. The system detects basic emotions and generates appropriate gesture responses, providing a practical platform for emotion-aware assistive robotics in autism therapy [3], [10].
