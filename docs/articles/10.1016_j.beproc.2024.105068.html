Incentive-salience attribution is attenuated in spontaneously hypertensive rats, an animal model of ADHD.

González-Barriga, Fernanda, Orduña, Vladimir  ·  Behavioural processes  ·  2024
ASD + ADHD
75/100This study directly relates to the profile's interest in ADHD-related reinforcement contingencies and motivational operations, using an established animal model to investigate incentive-salience attribution deficits that could inform behavioral interventions for ADHD populations.
Summary

This study investigated whether Spontaneously Hypertensive Rats (SHR), an animal model of ADHD, show deficits in incentive-salience attribution, a key component of motivation that has been understudied in this strain. The researchers used a Pavlovian Conditioned-Approach (PCA) task with 45 SHR and 45 Wistar control rats, where a lever was presented for 8 seconds before food pellet delivery, and incentive-salience attribution was measured by the PCA index incorporating lever responses, response latency, and response probability. SHR demonstrated significantly lower PCA levels compared to Wistar rats, indicating reduced attribution of incentive salience to the predictive stimulus. These findings replicate previous research and suggest that SHR exhibit motivational deficits consistent with ADHD characteristics.
