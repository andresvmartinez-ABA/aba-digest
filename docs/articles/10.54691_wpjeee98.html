Design and Preliminary Application Study of an AI Driven Personalized VR Rehabilitation Support System for Children with Autism

Guang-Ke Qi, Zhi-Liang Xia, Yin-Hui Yin  ·  Frontiers in Humanities and Social Sciences  ·  2026

Summary

# Summary: AI-Driven Personalized VR Rehabilitation Support System for Children with Autism This study aimed to address limitations in task adaptation, process transparency, and adjustment rationale for autism interventions by designing an AI-driven VR rehabilitation support system grounded in TEACCH structured teaching principles. The system integrated a large language model (DeepSeek), Unity virtual environments, and MQTT communication to enable ability profiling, strategy recommendation, training interaction, and multi-terminal coordination, with task adjustments verified through a closed-loop process involving candidate screening, output validation, and therapist confirmation. The preliminary application study produced a system design encompassing perceptual, communication, and social tasks organized into four functional modules (Explore at Your Own Pace, Fun Practice Zone, Everyday Scenarios Town, and Peer Play Community) with corresponding evaluation indicators, though only technical feasibility and preliminary acceptability data were assessed. The authors conclude that while the system design integrates structured tasks, AI recommendations, and professional oversight, definitive conclusions about rehabilitation effectiveness cannot be drawn until actual application data and training-related outcomes are collected and analyzed. BCBAs should note that this represents early-stage system development with pending empirical validation of clinical outcomes.

Abstract

Objective To address insufficient task adaptation, fragmented process information, and a lack of transparency in the basis for training adjustments for children with autism, this study designs an AI driven personalized VR rehabilitation support system and establishes a preliminary application evaluation framework. Methods Drawing on TEACCH structured teaching to organize tasks, the system integrates the DeepSeek large language model, Unity virtual environments, and MQTT communication to support ability profiling, strategy recommendation, training interaction, and coordination across multiple terminals. Candidate strategy screening, output validation, and therapist confirmation form a closed loop for task adjustment. Technical system tests and a small scale feasibility study are used to evaluate operational performance, training acceptability, and task performance. Results A system design covering perceptual, communication, and social tasks was developed, together with four functional modules—Explore at Your Own Pace, Fun Practice Zone, Everyday Scenarios Town, and Peer Play Community—and corresponding evaluation indicators. Conclusion This study proposes a VR rehabilitation support system that combines structured tasks, AI recommendations, and professional supervision. Actual system feasibility and training related outcomes must be assessed using application data; no definitive conclusion about rehabilitation effectiveness can yet be drawn.
