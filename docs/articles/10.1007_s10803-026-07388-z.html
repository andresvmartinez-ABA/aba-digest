Summary

# Summary: Video-Enhanced Activity Schedules for Young Children With Autism The study evaluated whether clinician-implemented video-enhanced activity schedules with embedded Premack contingencies improve on-task behavior, task independence, and reduce challenging behavior in preschool-aged children with autism. The research employed a concurrent multiple baseline across participants design with four preschool-aged children with ASD and four registered behavior technicians in a clinic setting. Results demonstrated a functional relation between the intervention and increased on-task behavior and independence; however, the functional relation for reducing challenging behavior was inconclusive, and social validity ratings from participants indicated the intervention was perceived as appropriate for instructional contexts. Video-enhanced activity schedules appear to be an effective instructional modification for increasing on-task behavior and independence in young children with ASD, suggesting potential utility for BCBAs implementing preacademic interventions in clinic-based settings. Future research is needed to clarify the intervention's effects on challenging behavior reduction and to evaluate its effectiveness across diverse settings and participant populations.

Abstract

PURPOSE: The purpose of this study was to evaluate the effects of a clinician-implemented video-enhanced activity schedule and an embedded Premack contingency on on-task behavior, independent task completion, and challenging behavior for preschool-aged children with ASD. METHOD: Participants included four preschool-aged children with ASD and four registered behavior technicians in one clinic. The study utilized a concurrent multiple baseline across participants design to evaluate the effects of the video-enhanced activity schedule on child on-task behavior, independence, and challenging behavior. RESULTS: Results indicated a functional relation between the implementation of the intervention and an increase in on-task behavior and independence; however, the results suggest a functional relation was inconclusive for reductions of challenging behavior. Social validity results indicated that participants perceived the intervention to be socially valid for instructional contexts. CONCLUSIONS: Study findings suggest that video-enhanced activity schedules may be an effective instructional modification for increasing on-task behavior and independence for young children with ASD. Implications for future research and practice are discussed.

Introduction

Autism spectrum disorders (ASD) is a neurodevelopmental disability (Hirota & King,2023) characterized by deficits in social communication and interaction, as well as repetitive and restricted patterns of behaviors, interests, or activities (American Psychiatric Association [APA], 2022). Many young children with ASD (YC-ASD; approximately 2–5 years of age) also exhibit executive function (EF) difficulties, including working memory, response inhibition, and cognitive shifting (Demetriou et al.,2019). EF is a collection of cognitive processes that support goal-directed and future-oriented behavior, which are foundational for learning and development across diverse environments (Koechlin,2016). EF delays at a young age often persist into adulthood (Saulnier & Klaiman,2022) and are associated with challenges with adaptive behavior development and poorer long-term outcomes (Wallace et al.,2016). Importantly, deficits in core EF processes are associated with poor academic achievement (Metcalfe et al.,2013), especially for YC-ASD, who are already at higher risk for academic difficulties (Oswald et al.,2016).
