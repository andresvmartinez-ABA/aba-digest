Promoting sharing behaviours in children through the use of a customised novel computer system

Rachel Menzies  ·  ASAC  ·  2010
Technology in ABA
95/100Manually curated paper from Research Rabbit library.
Summary

This study aimed to develop a technology-enhanced learning environment to help children with Asperger Syndrome improve their sharing skills and increase social awareness and interaction. The researchers used a customized computer system featuring scenario-based learning, where each scenario focused on specific aspects of sharing behavior. The system was developed using a user-centered approach for implementation with children who experience social isolation. The authors expected that children using this system would become more socially networked individuals, with potential positive implications for learning and social interaction.

Abstract

Asperger Syndrome is a condition on the Autism Spectrum, characterised by a lack of ability to recognise and understand the thoughts, beliefs, desires and intentions of others. Thus, the ability to make sense of others' behaviours and predict their social actions is reduced. This paper aims to develop a technology-enhanced learning environment in which children with Asperger Syndrome can explore and improve their sharing skills. This may increase their social awareness and interaction. The system is scenario-based, with each scenario focussing on a specific aspect of sharing. It is expected that, through the user-centred development and implementation of this system, children who previously were socially isolated will be more capable of becoming more socially networked as individuals. This will have implications on learning and interaction.
