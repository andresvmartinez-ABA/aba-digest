Assessment and treatment of chronic hand mouthing.

Roscoe, Eileen M, Iwata, Brian A, Zhou, Liming  ·  Journal of applied behavior analysis  ·  2013
ASIB (Automatically Reinforced Self-Injurious Behavior)
85/100This article directly aligns with the profile's interest in automatically reinforced self-injurious behavior, functional analysis methodology, and behavioral assessment and treatment, though it focuses on institutional populations which may include adults.
Summary

This three-part study examined the assessment and treatment of hand mouthing (HM) behavior in individuals with intellectual disabilities. Study 1 assessed prevalence through interviews (N=802) and direct observation, finding rates of 12.7% and 8% respectively, with 39.1% of observed cases being self-injurious. Study 2 conducted modified functional analyses with 64 individuals, revealing that 98.4% of HM cases were maintained by automatic reinforcement. Study 3 implemented a progressive intervention sequence with 14 individuals, showing that noncontingent reinforcement (NCR) alone was effective for 6 participants, while combinations of NCR with differential reinforcement of alternative behavior and/or response blocking were needed for 7 others, and one participant required NCR plus brief manual restraint.
