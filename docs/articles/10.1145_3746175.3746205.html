Work-in-Progress: Exploring Generative AI in Social Robots for Children with Autism Spectrum Disorder

Inès Allag, Armand Manukyan, Jérôme Dinet, M. Rychalski  ·  European Conference on Cognitive Ergonomics  ·  2025

Summary

# Summary: Generative AI in Social Robots for Children with ASD The study investigates whether integrating generative AI (specifically ChatGPT-powered conversational modules) into social robots (Nao and Buddy) can enhance social communication skills, reasoning abilities, and educational engagement in children with ASD. The methodology involved a prospective design in which educators were interviewed to identify needs and objectives, with findings used to develop contextualized prompts and interaction scenarios for experimental pedagogical sessions conducted in classroom settings with 50 children aged 8–14 years diagnosed with ASD. The study employs both qualitative observations and quantitative assessments to analyze engagement levels, communicative behaviors, and problem-solving skills during child-robot interactions; preliminary findings are described as aiming to assess improvements in conversational engagement, social reciprocity, and motivation for educational tasks, though specific effect sizes or outcome data are not yet reported in this work-in-progress abstract. For BCBA practitioners, this research suggests potential applications of AI-enhanced social robots as supplementary tools to support social communication and educational engagement in school-based ABA programs, though evidence of efficacy remains preliminary and requires completion of data collection and analysis. The authors note that future work will focus on adaptive algorithm development, longitudinal impact assessment, and scalability, indicating that more rigorous evidence regarding clinical utility and generalizability across contexts is needed before broad implementation recommendations can be made.

Abstract

Autism spectrum disorder (ASD) presents complex challenges in social communication, emotional understanding, and adaptability to dynamic environments.Social robots have emerged as promising tools to support children with ASD by providing structured, predictable, and engaging interactions.Recent advances in generative artificial intelligence (AI) offer new opportunities to enhance the adaptability and responsiveness of these robots, enabling more naturalistic and personalized exchanges.This study investigates the integration of generative AI, specifically ChatGPT-powered conversational modules, into two social robots, Nao and Buddy, to evaluate their impact on the social communication skills, reasoning abilities, and educational engagement of children with ASD.Following a prospective design, interviews with educators identified the needs and objectives pertinent to the children.These insights informed the development of contextualized prompts and interaction scenarios.Experimental pedagogical sessions will be conducted in classroom settings with fifty children aged 8 to 14 years diagnosed with ASD.Qualitative observations and quantitative assessments will be employed to analyze engagement levels, communicative behaviors, and problem-solving skills during child-robot interactions.Preliminary findings aim to assess how AI-enhanced robots can foster greater conversational engagement, improved social reciprocity, and increased motivation for educational tasks.The study underscores the potential of combining social robotics with generative AI to support the autonomy and inclusion of neurodivergent learners.Future work will focus on adaptive algorithm development, longitudinal impact assessment, and scalability across diverse educational contexts.
