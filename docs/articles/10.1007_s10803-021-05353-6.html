Inhibitory Control in Autism Spectrum Disorders: Meta-analyses on Indirect and Direct Measures.

Tonizzi, Irene, Giofrè, David, Usai, Maria Carmen  ·  Journal of autism and developmental disorders  ·  2022
ASD + ADHD
72/100This meta-analysis on inhibitory control in autism spectrum disorders directly aligns with the BCBA's interests in behavioral assessment, ADHD-ASD comorbidity, and research on autism populations, though it focuses more on cognitive research than applied behavioral interventions.
Summary

This meta-analytic study examined inhibitory control (IC) deficits in individuals with autism spectrum disorders (ASD) compared to neurotypical controls. The researchers conducted two separate meta-analyses: one analyzing 164 studies using direct IC measures (n = 5,140 ASD participants, n = 6,075 controls) and another analyzing 24 studies using indirect measures (n = 985 ASD participants, n = 1,300 controls). Results showed a significant small-to-medium effect size (g = 0.484) for IC deficits on direct measures and a large effect size (g = 1.334) on indirect measures in the ASD group. The findings indicated that intellectual functioning, age, and presentation format served as significant moderators of IC performance, while comorbid ADHD did not significantly impact results.
