Summary

# Summary: Socio-Communication Skill Deficits in ASD in Early Childhood This thematic literature review examined common socio-communication skill deficits in young children with autism spectrum disorder and identified evidence-based intervention strategies appropriate for speech-language therapists and caregivers, with particular focus on multilingual and low-resource contexts. The authors conducted a systematic thematic synthesis of over 35 peer-reviewed studies to characterize deficits in joint attention, social reciprocity, pragmatic language, nonverbal communication, and social cognition across diagnostic frameworks (DSM-IV-TR and DSM-5). The review identified five primary evidence-based intervention approaches: Naturalistic Developmental Behavioral Interventions, pragmatic language therapy, caregiver-mediated interventions, peer-mediated approaches, and augmentative and alternative communication, while highlighting methodological limitations and research gaps, particularly in African and multilingual settings. BCBAs and autism practitioners should prioritize culturally responsive, family-centered, and contextually adaptable socio-communication interventions during early childhood, recognizing that intervention strategies must be tailored to diverse linguistic and resource-constrained environments. This synthesis underscores the critical need for additional research in underrepresented geographic and cultural contexts to inform more broadly applicable clinical practice.

Abstract

Autism spectrum disorder is a lifelong neurological condition that interferes with socio-communication skills of an individual. It is also characterized by repetitive and restrictive activities or/and interests. Socio-communication deficits are a hallmark feature of the condition and are particularly consequential during early childhood, a critical period for language and social development. This systematic thematic review synthesizes empirical evidence from over 35 peer-reviewed studies published in high-impact international journals to examine common socio-communication skill deficits in young children with ASD and evidence-based intervention strategies appropriate for speech-language therapists and caregivers, with specific attention to multilingual and low-resource contexts. Drawing on diagnostic frameworks from DSM-IV-TR and DSM-5, the review highlights deficits in joint attention, social reciprocity, pragmatic language, nonverbal communication, and social cognition. Intervention approaches reviewed include Naturalistic Developmental Behavioral Interventions, pragmatic language therapy, caregiver-mediated interventions, peer-mediated approaches, and augmentative and alternative communication. Critical synthesis identifies areas of consensus, methodological limitations, research gaps in African and multilingual settings, and implications for clinical practice and future research. Findings underscore the need for culturally responsive, family-centered, and contextually adaptable socio-communication interventions in early childhood.
