Summary

# Summary: Edge-Based Robot-Assisted Hand Gesture Recognition for Autism Intervention The primary objective of this research was to develop a real-time, vision-based framework that autonomously supports gesture learning and correction for children with ASD without requiring therapist intervention or intrusive wearable sensors. The methodology employed a MediaPipe-based skeletal landmark extraction system with a hybrid keyframe selection strategy combining entropy-based filtering and clustering mechanisms, spatial feature modeling using one-dimensional convolutions, and contrastive learning—implemented on the CARAT (Caregiver Autism Robot with AI-based Technology) platform running on edge computing hardware (Jetson Orin Nano). The framework achieved high recognition accuracy across participant groups: 98.85% with healthy children, 96.42% with autistic children, and 97.41% on the combined dataset, while simultaneously performing gesture classification and correctness evaluation with real-time verbal and physical feedback. For BCBAs and autism practitioners, this system presents a potential tool to augment gesture instruction and social skill training through autonomous, real-time feedback delivery in resource-constrained settings, though further research on generalization across diverse environments and comparative efficacy with standard ABA-based gesture instruction is warranted.

Abstract

Children with Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) often face challenges in expressing and interpreting social gestures essential for social skill development. Traditional approaches depend on intrusive wearable sensors and manual supervision, with minimal adaptation to real-time, resource-constrained environments. To address these limitations, we present a real-time vision-based human–robot interaction (HRI) framework that autonomously supports gesture learning and correction without therapist intervention. The system employs MediaPipe-based skeletal landmark extraction with a novel hybrid keyframe selection strategy. This strategy integrates entropy-based filtering to retain semantically rich frames and a clustering-based mechanism to identify representative frames from similar gesture groups. Spatial features are modeled using one-dimensional convolutions across hand joints to capture intra-frame structural dependencies. The framework executes gesture classification and correctness evaluation simultaneously, with contrastive learning incorporated to enhance the separability of learned embeddings. An in-house assistive robot, named CARAT (Caregiver Autism Robot with AI-based Technology), has been developed to realize this framework. CARAT is equipped with a gesture-performing arm, deployed on a Jetson Orin Nano edge device, and provides real-time verbal feedback alongside physical gesture demonstrations. The dataset for evaluation was collected using CARAT, involving participants from both healthy children and children with ASD. The framework demonstrates strong recognition performance, achieving 98.85% accuracy with healthy participants, 96.42% with autistic participants, and 97.41% on the combined dataset of both groups. These results validate the effectiveness of the proposed approach for hand gesture recognition in edge-deployed assistive robots for autism intervention.
