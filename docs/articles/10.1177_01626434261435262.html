Enhancing Daily Living Skills in Adolescents with Autism: The Role of Artificial Intelligence-Assisted Problem-Solving Instruction

Esra Orum Çattık  ·  Journal of Special Education Technology  ·  2026

Summary

# Summary: AI-Assisted Problem-Solving Instruction for Adolescents with Autism The study examined the effectiveness of artificial intelligence-assisted instruction on problem-solving skills during daily living tasks in adolescents with autism spectrum disorder. The researchers used a multiple-probe design with three male adolescents (ages 14–17) who received structured AI-supported instruction via iPad, incorporating task analysis, errorless instruction, visual cues, and problem-solving scenarios with missing, incorrect, or inconsistent materials. All three participants demonstrated increased rates of independent problem-solving in daily life tasks, successfully generalized problem-solving strategies to novel scenarios, and maintained acquired skills at 2-, 4-, and 6-week follow-up intervals; social validity data indicated the intervention was meaningful and applicable according to both participants and expert raters. BCBAs and autism practitioners may consider AI-supported problem-solving instruction as a potentially effective supplementary approach for teaching daily living skills and promoting generalization and maintenance of problem-solving strategies in adolescents with ASD.

Abstract

The purpose of this study is to examine the effect of artificial intelligence (AI)life tasks. A multiple-probe design was used in this study. The participants included three male adolescents aged 14–17 with ASD. During the teaching process, a structured AI-supported activity program based on task analysis, errorless instruction, and visual cues was used via iPad. Problem-solving strategies were taught through scenarios created with missing, incorrect, and inconsistent materials. Data were collected through video recordings during the baseline, intervention, generalization, and maintenance sessions; the dependent variable was the participants’ rates of demonstrating correct problem-solving responses in problematic tasks. The findings showed that all participants increased their ability to independently solve problems that arose during daily life tasks at the end of the teaching process, were able to generalize problem-solving strategies to new scenarios, and were able to maintain the skills they acquired after the teaching ended for 2, 4, and 6 weeks. Social validity data indicated that the instructional process was considered meaningful, useful, and applicable to both participants and experts. The study demonstrates that AI-supported problem-solving strategies are an effective, valid, and socially acceptable instructional approach for supporting daily life tasks in adolescents with ASD.
