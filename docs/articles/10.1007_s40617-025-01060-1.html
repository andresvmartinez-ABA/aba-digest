The Potential Evocative Effect of Leisure Item Omission in the Attention and Escape Conditions of a Functional Analysis.

Melanson, Isaac J, Fahmie, Tara A, Kastner, Kendall M  ·  Behavior analysis in practice  ·  2026
FTF Authors
100/100[FTF author +25 → 100] Directly addresses functional analysis methodology, reinforcer assessment, and stimulus control of stereotypy/problem behavior—core interests in the profile—with clear clinical application and practical implications for behavioral assessment design.
Summary

# Summary: Leisure Item Omission in Functional Analysis The study investigated whether the absence of leisure items (rather than the presence of attention or escape contingencies) could evoke challenging behavior during functional analyses in individuals who typically request preferred items. The researchers conducted functional analyses with two participants under standard conditions, followed by modified conditions that included preferred leisure items in attention or escape phases, and a component analysis to isolate the key variables. Both participants showed elevated target behavior and leisure item requests when preferred items were omitted from attention or escape conditions; when leisure items were added to these conditions, target behavior decreased to near-zero levels, and the component analysis confirmed that item omission (not the absence of the programmed establishing operation) accounted for the elevated responding. BCBAs should consider that challenging behavior during functional analysis may be evoked by the omission of preferred items rather than by the programmed establishing operations and should routinely measure requests and behavior occurring when establishing operations are absent to improve functional analysis accuracy and clinical decision-making.

Abstract

The potential suppressive effects of leisure items in the attention condition of functional analyses have been documented (Ringdahl et al., 2002; Roscoe et al., 2008). However, the potential evocative effects of their absence have not been as thoroughly explored. The current study evaluated the impact of the omission of leisure items in a standard attention or escape condition of a functional analysis. Two participants exhibited leisure item requests and target behavior when the programmed establishing operation for attention or escape was absent during an initial functional analysis of their target behavior. This pattern of responding led to a modification of conditions in which preferred leisure items were included in the attention or escape condition for both participants, resulting in the absence of target behavior. A follow-up component analysis verified that the omission of leisure items and not the programmed establishing operation were responsible for the higher levels of responding in the respective test conditions. Results are discussed in the context of decision-making processes in functional analysis design; a recommendation is made to adopt routine measures of requests and programmed-establishing-operation-absent behavior.
